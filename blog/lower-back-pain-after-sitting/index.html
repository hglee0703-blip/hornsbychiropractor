Lower Back Pain After Sitting: Practical Relief

13 September 2026 · Lower back pain

It is late afternoon, you stand up from your desk and your lower back feels tight, achy or reluctant to straighten. The first few steps may be awkward. Often, the sensible response is not a hard stretch or a punishing workout, but a little comfortable movement and a change of position.

Why a desk day can stir up your back

Sitting at work has not, by itself, been established as a direct cause of lower back pain. A systematic review found that the available evidence did not support sitting alone as an independent cause (PubMed). That does not mean your chair is irrelevant. An already sensitive back may become uncomfortable when you stay in one position for a long time.

Pain after sitting does not automatically mean a disc has slipped or your spine is damaged. Back pain has many possible causes, and in many cases the precise source cannot be identified from symptoms alone (healthdirect). Notice what changes the pain rather than trying to hold a supposedly perfect posture all day.

What to do when you finish work

Start with gentle movement. Walk around the room or outside at a comfortable pace for five to ten minutes. If that feels manageable, try a few slow movements rather than forcing your back to stretch as far as possible.

  • Pelvic rocking: lying on your back with your knees bent, gently flatten and release your lower back.
  • Sit-to-stand: rise from a firm chair and sit again with control, using your hands if needed.
  • Standing back movement: place your hands on your hips and lean backwards slightly, but only if this feels comfortable.
  • Easy walking: keep the pace relaxed and let your arms move naturally.

There is no need to push through sharp pain. Stop an exercise if it causes increasing pain down the leg, new tingling, numbness or weakness. Regular exercise can help people with ongoing non-specific lower back pain, although the most suitable type and dose differ between individuals (Cochrane review via PubMed). A wrapped heat pack may also be soothing; follow its instructions and protect your skin from burns. Heat and cold packs are among the self-care options discussed by healthdirect.

Make the next desk day less provocative

A useful workstation is adjustable, supported and easy to move away from. Bring the chair close enough that you are not reaching for the keyboard. Keep your feet supported, relax your shoulders and place the screen roughly in front of you. A small rolled towel behind the lower back is reasonable if it feels better, but it should not force you into an uncomfortable arch.

The position you use matters less than whether you can change it. Safe Work Australia recommends reducing and regularly breaking up prolonged sitting (Safe Work Australia). Choose a reminder you will actually follow. You might stand during a phone call, refill your water or take a brief walk between tasks.

Laptop work on a sofa or at a kitchen bench can be difficult because the screen and keyboard are joined. For longer sessions, consider raising the laptop screen and using a separate keyboard and mouse. Alternate between sitting and standing if you wish, but do not simply replace hours of sitting with hours of rigid standing.

If the pain travels into your leg

Pain that runs through the buttock and down one leg, especially with tingling, numbness or weakness, can occur when a spinal nerve is irritated or compressed (Mayo Clinic). Avoid repeatedly stretching into electrical, burning or spreading leg pain. A clinical assessment can check strength, sensation and reflexes and help decide whether further investigation is warranted.

When to arrange an assessment

Book an appointment with a qualified health professional if the pain is worsening, keeps returning, limits normal activity or is not beginning to settle. Assessment is also sensible after a significant fall or accident, or if you have unexplained weight loss, fever, a history of cancer or pain that is consistently worse at night. These features do not prove there is a serious problem, but they deserve medical review (Mayo Clinic).

Seek urgent medical care for new loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness around the genitals or inner thighs, or rapidly worsening weakness in one or both legs. These can be warning signs of significant nerve compression (healthdirect). If symptoms are severe or you are unsure what to do in Australia, call healthdirect on 1800 022 222; call 000 for an emergency.

Frequently asked questions

These are common practical questions about desk-related lower back discomfort.

What is the best sitting posture for lower back pain?

There is no single position that everyone must hold. Aim for a supported posture that feels comfortable, then change it regularly. Keep frequently used items within easy reach so you are not repeatedly twisting or leaning forward.

How often should I get up from my desk?

There is no universal interval that suits every person or job. Safe Work Australia advises regularly breaking up prolonged sitting (Safe Work Australia). Pick a realistic reminder and take a brief movement break before discomfort becomes intense.

Should I use heat or ice after sitting?

Either may be tried if it feels comfortable, although neither addresses every cause of back pain. Wrap the pack, follow the product instructions and avoid direct contact with the skin. Stop if it irritates the area.

Can chiropractic care help desk-related lower back pain?

Chiropractic care may include assessment, advice, exercise and manual treatment. A Cochrane review found spinal manipulation for chronic lower back pain was generally no better or worse than other established therapies, so it should not be presented as a guaranteed stand-alone fix (Cochrane review via PubMed). Suitability depends on your examination findings, preferences and medical history.
