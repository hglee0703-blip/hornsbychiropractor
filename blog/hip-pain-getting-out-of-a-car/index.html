Hip Pain Getting Out of a Car: Causes and Relief

16 November 2026 · Andy Lee, Chiropractor · Hip pain

You park, release the seatbelt, swing your legs towards the door and feel a sharp catch as you stand. Hip pain getting out of a car often appears during the combination of turning, lifting the leg and taking weight through it. The exact location and timing can offer useful clues, although they cannot confirm the cause on their own.

Hip pain may arise from the joint, muscles, tendons or bursae around it. Pain referred from the lower back can also be felt near the hip or buttock (healthdirect). That is why two people with similar pain during a car exit may need quite different approaches.

Why hip pain getting out of a car happens

A typical car seat holds the hip in a bent position. To get out, you then rotate the thigh, move the leg sideways and shift your body weight onto one foot. A low seat or narrow door opening can make the movement more awkward. If a joint or nearby soft tissue is already irritated, that sequence may bring the pain on.

How long you have been sitting can matter too. Osteoarthritis commonly causes stiffness after rest, while irritated tendons or bursae may hurt when they are compressed or loaded (Mayo Clinic; Mayo Clinic). The car movement may therefore expose an existing problem rather than being its original cause.

Common causes and their usual patterns

Several conditions can produce pain during this movement. The following patterns are a guide, not a diagnosis:

  • Hip osteoarthritis: Pain is often felt in the groin or around the hip and may be accompanied by stiffness or reduced movement. Symptoms can become more noticeable after sitting or with weight-bearing activity (Mayo Clinic).
  • Greater trochanteric pain or bursitis: Pain over the outside of the hip may be tender to touch and can be aggravated by lying on that side, climbing stairs or standing from a low position (Mayo Clinic). If night-time discomfort is part of the picture, this guide to hip pain while lying on your side may also be useful.
  • Muscle or tendon strain: A recent change in exercise, a sudden slip or an awkward leg movement can strain tissue around the hip. Strains may cause local pain, tenderness, swelling or difficulty using the affected muscle (healthdirect).
  • Labral irritation or injury: The labrum is cartilage around the rim of the hip socket. A labral tear may cause groin pain, stiffness, clicking or a catching feeling, although some tears cause no symptoms (Mayo Clinic).
  • Referred pain from the lower back: Irritation of a spinal nerve can produce pain in the buttock, hip region or leg, sometimes with tingling, numbness or weakness (Mayo Clinic). This is discussed further in the article on disc protrusion and sciatica.

These problems can overlap. Someone with mild joint stiffness may also have sensitive outer-hip tendons, for example. A physical examination is often more useful than trying to name the problem from one movement alone.

Clues from where and when it hurts

Groin pain or a deep pinch at the front of the hip tends to raise more suspicion of the hip joint itself. Pain directly over the bony outside of the hip points more towards local tendons or a bursa, particularly if lying on that side also hurts. Buttock pain that travels down the thigh, especially with pins and needles or numbness, may be related to the lower back or a nerve (healthdirect; healthdirect).

Also notice which part of the exit triggers the pain. Is it lifting the knee, turning the thigh, placing the foot down or pushing up to stand? Check whether walking for a minute eases the stiffness, or whether each step makes it worse. Those details are worth recording if the problem persists.

What to try before your next drive

Change the exit rather than forcing the same painful twist. Slide the seat back before opening the door. Turn your trunk and legs together so that both feet face out of the car, then move towards the edge of the seat. Lean forward slightly and push through your legs and hands to stand. If this is more comfortable, use it while the area settles.

On longer drives, take sensible breaks and change position when safe to do so. Gentle, regular movement is generally preferred to prolonged rest for many musculoskeletal problems, provided it does not cause marked or lasting pain (healthdirect). A short walk after getting out may feel better than immediately carrying bags or climbing stairs.

Exercise should match the likely source of the pain. A comfortable bridge, sit-to-stand or supported hip movement may be reasonable, but stop if it creates sharp groin pain, catching or increasing symptoms down the leg. Aggressive stretching is not automatically helpful. If the front of the hip simply feels stiff rather than sharply painful, see these gentle hip flexor stretching options. A pharmacist or GP can advise whether pain medicine is suitable for you, particularly if you have other health conditions or take regular medicines (healthdirect).

When the hip should be assessed

Arrange an assessment if the pain keeps returning, is getting worse, affects sleep or walking, or limits ordinary tasks. An examination may include hip and lower-back movement, strength, walking, tenderness and a basic nerve check. Scans are not required for every episode of hip pain, but a clinician may recommend imaging when the history or examination suggests it would change management (healthdirect).

Seek prompt medical care after a significant fall or injury if you cannot put weight through the leg, the joint appears deformed, or the pain is intense. Fever with a hot, swollen joint also needs urgent assessment (Mayo Clinic). New bladder or bowel trouble, numbness around the groin, or major leg weakness alongside back or leg pain requires emergency attention (healthdirect).

For non-urgent pain, a GP, physiotherapist or chiropractor can assess whether the main issue appears to be the hip, surrounding tissue or lower back. You can also read about chiropractic care in Hornsby and what an appointment involves before deciding what type of care suits you.

Frequently asked questions

Does hip pain getting out of the car mean arthritis?

No. Osteoarthritis is one possibility, but tendons, bursae, muscles, the hip labrum and referred lower-back pain can produce similar symptoms (healthdirect). The location, history and physical examination matter more than one painful movement.

Should I stretch a painful hip?

Only if the movement feels comfortable and does not create sharp pain, catching or worsening symptoms. Stretching may suit simple stiffness, but it can irritate some joint and tendon problems, so persistent pain should be assessed rather than repeatedly forced.

Which leg should come out of the car first?

There is no universal rule. Turning both legs out together usually avoids leaving one hip twisted behind; from there, use the sequence that feels steadier and less painful. Take extra care if balance is poor.

Can sciatica feel like hip pain?

Yes. Sciatica can cause pain through the buttock and leg and may include tingling, numbness or weakness (healthdirect). Groin pain and restricted hip movement are less typical, so an examination may be needed when the source is unclear.
