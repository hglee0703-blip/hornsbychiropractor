Neck and shoulder pain while breastfeeding

27 September 2026 · Neck & posture

It is 2 am, the baby has finally latched, and you realise your chin has been tucked towards your chest for the past ten minutes. One shoulder is raised, the other arm is holding most of the baby's weight, and moving feels likely to disturb the feed.

This sort of position is easy to slip into, especially during long or frequent feeds. A few changes to how you support yourself and your baby can make feeding more comfortable without chasing a rigid idea of perfect posture.

Why feeding can aggravate your neck and shoulders

Neck pain is commonly affected by sustained positions, muscle strain and repeated movements (healthdirect). During breastfeeding, the problem is often less about one harmful posture and more about staying still while looking down, reaching forward or holding an arm without enough support.

The aim is not to sit bolt upright for every feed. That usually becomes tiring in its own way. Instead, look for a position in which your back, forearms and baby are supported, your shoulders can drop, and you can change position before discomfort becomes sharp or distracting.

Set yourself up before the baby latches

Gather what you need first: water, a pillow or folded towel, and something to support your feet if they do not rest comfortably on the floor. Sit back into the chair rather than perching on its edge. Bring your baby towards your breast instead of bending your upper body towards the baby.

There is no single correct breastfeeding position. Cradle, cross-cradle, football hold, laid-back and side-lying positions can all be useful depending on your comfort, your baby's age and how feeding is going (Pregnancy, Birth and Baby).

  • Support the baby's weight. Place pillows under the baby and your forearm, rather than using your shoulder to hold the arm up.
  • Bring the feed higher. If you keep bending your neck to reach the baby, add support underneath rather than stacking pillows behind your neck.
  • Relax your grip. Once the latch is settled, check whether you are unnecessarily clenching your hand, jaw or raised shoulder.
  • Change sides and positions. You do not have to use exactly the same chair or hold at every feed.

If side-lying feeding helps, plan for what happens if you become sleepy. Babies should be returned to their own firm, flat sleep surface, and falling asleep with a baby on a sofa or armchair should be avoided (healthdirect).

Use small movements between feeds

You do not need a long exercise session each time. Brief, comfortable movement after feeding may be more realistic. A Cochrane review found that some strengthening and endurance exercises may help persistent mechanical neck pain, although the quality and results of the evidence varied (PubMed).

Try one or two of the following, moving slowly and staying within a comfortable range:

  • Turn your head gently left and right, without forcing the end of the movement.
  • Draw your shoulder blades slightly back and down, then let them relax.
  • Place your hands behind your head and lean your upper back over the back of a supportive chair, provided this feels comfortable.
  • Open and close your hands, then circle your wrists after a feed.
  • Take a short walk around the room or home instead of remaining in the feeding chair.

Stop an exercise if it causes dizziness, a strong headache, pain spreading down the arm, numbness or weakness. Exercises should feel gentle rather than like a test of how far you can stretch.

Heat, pain relief and hands-on care

A warm pack may settle a stiff neck or shoulder for some people. Wrap it, follow the product instructions and check the skin regularly. Heat is one of the simple self-care measures suggested for uncomplicated neck pain (healthdirect).

If you are considering pain relief, ask a pharmacist, GP or other qualified prescriber what is suitable for you and your baby. Medicines can pass into breastmilk in different amounts, so the choice and dose need to take account of the particular medicine and your health circumstances (healthdirect). Do not rely on a medication being available without a prescription as proof that it is appropriate while breastfeeding.

If pain keeps returning, interferes with feeding or is not settling, an assessment can help clarify whether the main issue is the neck, shoulder, upper back or a combination. In clinic, I would also ask about headaches, arm symptoms, previous injuries, feeding positions and what movements change the pain. Care may include practical positioning advice, suitable exercises and hands-on treatment where appropriate, rather than treatment being based on posture alone.

Know when neck or shoulder pain needs prompt attention

Seek medical advice promptly if neck pain follows a significant injury, is severe or worsening, or occurs with fever, loss of balance, arm weakness, persistent numbness or other neurological symptoms. These features warrant assessment rather than repeated stretching or massage at home (healthdirect).

A severe headache after childbirth, particularly with visual changes, high blood pressure, shortness of breath or pain below the ribs, needs urgent medical assessment because post-partum pre-eclampsia can occur after delivery (Mayo Clinic). Fever with a painful, red or swollen area of the breast may be mastitis and should also be discussed promptly with a health professional (healthdirect). Call 000 for severe or life-threatening symptoms.

This article provides general information only. It is not a diagnosis or individual treatment advice. Please consult a qualified health professional if you have persistent pain, concerning symptoms or questions about exercises or medicines.

Frequently asked questions

These are common practical questions about feeding-related neck and shoulder discomfort.

Should I look down while my baby is feeding?

Looking down briefly to check the latch is reasonable. The problem is often staying there for the entire feed. Once feeding is settled, let your head return to a comfortable position and use your eyes, rather than your whole neck, to glance down.

Is side-lying breastfeeding better for neck pain?

It can reduce the amount of time spent sitting and holding the baby's weight, but it will not suit everyone. If you might fall asleep, return your baby to a separate, firm and flat sleep surface and avoid feeding on a sofa or armchair (healthdirect).

Can I use a heat pack while breastfeeding?

A warm pack may be used on a stiff neck or shoulder if you follow its safety instructions, wrap it and check your skin. Do not place it where it could overheat or come into direct contact with your baby.

Can a chiropractor help with breastfeeding-related neck pain?

An appointment may be reasonable if the pain persists or keeps interfering with feeding. Assessment should consider your neck, shoulders, upper back, arm symptoms and feeding setup, with referral for medical care if the findings suggest that is needed.
