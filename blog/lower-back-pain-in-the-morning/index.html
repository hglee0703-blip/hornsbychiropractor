Why Does My Lower Back Hurt in the Morning?

19 September 2026 · Lower back pain

The first few steps out of bed can be surprisingly uncomfortable. Your lower back feels tight, putting on socks is awkward, and standing upright takes a minute. Then, after a shower and some movement, it may start to settle.

Morning pain is a timing pattern rather than a diagnosis. It can be related to an irritated muscle, joint or disc, but persistent morning stiffness can occasionally point to an inflammatory condition or another health problem (Mayo Clinic). The way it behaves during the rest of the day often provides useful clues.

Why your back may feel worse after sleep

Your spine has spent several hours in much the same position. If an area is already sensitive, sustained bending, twisting or arching during sleep may aggravate it. Muscles, spinal joints and intervertebral discs can all contribute to lower back pain, and more than one structure may be involved at the same time (healthdirect).

Spinal discs also change slightly through the day. They take up fluid while the spine is unloaded overnight and lose some of that fluid during normal daytime loading, which is one reason disc-related symptoms can vary with posture and time of day (PubMed). This does not mean that morning pain proves you have a damaged or slipped disc.

Your mattress and sleeping position may play a part, although there is no single mattress that suits every back. One clinical trial found that a medium-firm mattress produced better outcomes than a firm mattress for people with chronic non-specific lower back pain, but personal comfort and support still matter (PubMed).

Patterns that can give us a clue

Mechanical back pain commonly changes with posture, lifting and movement, and may arise from strains, age-related joint changes or disc irritation (Mayo Clinic). If your back is sore on waking but loosens gradually as you walk around, the overnight position and reduced movement may be contributing. Pain after sleeping on a different bed or after an unusually physical day also fits this broad pattern, though it cannot confirm the cause.

Pain travelling through the buttock and down one leg, especially with tingling, numbness or weakness, may involve irritation of a sciatic nerve root (healthdirect). Disc protrusions are only one possible cause, and disc changes seen on a scan do not always explain a person’s pain (PubMed). Our article on disc protrusion, sciatica and treatment options covers this in more depth.

Inflammatory back pain tends to behave differently. Conditions such as ankylosing spondylitis can cause pain and stiffness that are worse after rest or during the night and improve with activity (Mayo Clinic). This pattern does not automatically mean you have an inflammatory condition, but prolonged morning stiffness, repeated night pain, or symptoms affecting other joints deserve a medical assessment.

What to try over the next few mornings

Start with gentle movement rather than forcing a deep stretch. A short walk around the house, comfortable pelvic movements or a warm shower may be easier than trying to touch your toes straight away. Staying active within tolerable limits is generally preferred to prolonged bed rest for uncomplicated lower back pain (healthdirect), and exercise can help some people with ongoing non-specific lower back pain (Cochrane).

Review your sleeping set-up as well. Side sleepers may find a pillow between the knees more comfortable, while people sleeping on their back may prefer a pillow beneath the knees to reduce strain on the lower back (Mayo Clinic). Before replacing a mattress, check whether it is sagging, whether your pillow position twists your trunk, and whether the symptoms change in another supportive bed.

Keep a brief note for a week: how long the stiffness lasts, whether pain enters the leg, and which positions help or aggravate it. If you suspect disc or sciatic irritation, avoid choosing an exercise solely because it is labelled a back exercise. Our guide to practical lumbar disc management discusses walking, sitting, driving and McKenzie-style movements, but an exercise that helps one presentation can aggravate another. For pain medicines, check with a pharmacist or GP because anti-inflammatory medicines such as ibuprofen are not suitable for everyone (healthdirect).

When morning back pain needs assessment

Arrange an assessment if the pain keeps returning, is getting worse, disrupts sleep repeatedly, spreads below the knee, or is accompanied by numbness or weakness. A qualified clinician can check your history, spinal movement, strength, reflexes and sensation, then decide whether further medical investigation is warranted. Imaging is not routinely required for uncomplicated lower back pain, and scan findings need to be considered alongside the examination (healthdirect).

Seek urgent medical care for new loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness around the genitals or inner thighs, or rapidly worsening weakness in both legs, as these can occur with cauda equina syndrome (NCBI Bookshelf). Back pain following significant trauma, or pain accompanied by fever, unexplained weight loss or a history of cancer, also needs prompt medical assessment (Mayo Clinic). Pain higher in the side or back with fever, nausea or urinary symptoms may come from the urinary system rather than the spine (healthdirect).

Frequently asked questions

These are common practical questions about waking with a sore lower back.

Is morning lower back pain caused by my mattress?

A mattress can contribute if it leaves your trunk poorly supported or keeps you in an uncomfortable position, but it is rarely the only possible explanation. Research suggests medium-firm mattresses may suit some people with chronic non-specific lower back pain better than firm mattresses (PubMed). Check for sagging and consider whether your pain changes when you sleep elsewhere before buying a replacement.

How long should morning back stiffness last?

There is no single normal duration. Stiffness that eases after gentle movement can occur with mechanical back pain, while prolonged stiffness accompanied by night pain may warrant assessment for an inflammatory cause (Mayo Clinic). The trend over several days is often more useful than one morning.

Should I stretch as soon as I get out of bed?

Gentle, comfortable movement is usually a better starting point than forceful stretching. Staying active is generally recommended for uncomplicated back pain, but movements that increase leg pain, numbness or weakness should be stopped and assessed (healthdirect). A short walk may be enough to see how the back responds.

Could morning back pain be a slipped disc?

It could involve a disc, particularly if pain travels into a leg, but morning pain by itself cannot identify the source. Disc protrusions are also found on scans in some people without back pain, so imaging must be interpreted carefully (PubMed). Seek assessment if leg symptoms persist or weakness develops.
