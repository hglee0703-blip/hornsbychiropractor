Sciatica Treatment Options: A Hornsby Chiro's Guide

31 August 2026 · Sciatica & Lower Back Pain

Disclaimer: The information provided in this article is for general educational purposes only and is not intended as personal medical advice or diagnosis. Always consult a qualified health professional, such as a chiropractor or GP, before starting any new treatment plan.

If you have ever felt a sharp, shooting pain radiating from your lower back down through your buttock and into your leg, you know how agonizing sciatica can be. In my Hornsby clinic, sciatica is easily one of the most painful conditions patients bring through the door. Some days, even sitting down to eat dinner or driving to the local shops feels almost impossible.

The good news? The vast majority of sciatica cases improve without invasive procedures. The tricky part is figuring out which path to take. With options ranging from simple movement strategies to spinal surgery, it helps to understand what the evidence actually says about each approach.

What Is Sciatica Really?

Sciatica is not a standalone diagnosis. It is a set of symptoms caused by irritation or compression of the sciatic nerve, which is the largest nerve in your body. This nerve originates in your lower spine, travels through your pelvis, and extends right down into your foot (healthdirect, 2024).

When a nerve root gets pinched or inflamed—most commonly by a bulging or herniated disc—you might feel burning pain, tingling, numbness, or even weakness in the leg. If you are dealing with sharp pain aggravated by sitting or bending forward, reviewing proper lumbar disc injury management strategies is usually a critical first step in keeping symptoms manageable while the area heals.

First-Line Conservative Care: Active Recovery

Years ago, standard medical advice for sciatica was strict bed rest. We now know that was a mistake. Lying in bed for days on end actually delays recovery and causes the surrounding postural muscles to weaken (Cochrane Review, 2010). Pain makes you want to lie still, but controlled movement is almost always better.

In my clinical experience, gentle, short walks spread throughout the day work wonders. You do not need to walk for hours. Ten minutes every two hours helps pump fluid around the spinal structures, reduces joint stiffness, and prevents nerve sensitivity from setting in. If an activity sharply increases leg pain, stop and modify it, but try hard to avoid total immobility.

Chiropractic Care and Physical Rehabilitation

For most patients in Hornsby who come to see me with nerve pain, conservative manual care is the primary starting point. Spinal manipulation, soft-tissue mobilization, and precise joint adjustments aim to improve spinal biomechanics and relieve mechanical stress on irritated nerve tissue. Systematic reviews demonstrate that spinal manual therapy can provide meaningful relief for acute back and leg pain (PubMed, 2010).

Alongside hands-on therapy, targeted exercise plays a major role. Exercises like McKenzie extension protocols and gentle nerve flossing help encourage the nerve to slide smoothly through surrounding tissues. Surprisingly, many patients do not realise that lumbar disc herniations frequently heal on their own. High-quality systematic reviews show that spontaneous regression of herniated disc material occurs naturally in a significant percentage of cases (PubMed, 2014). Physical rehabilitation helps keep your spine mobile while your body takes care of that natural breakdown and reabsorption process.

Medications and Epidural Cortisone Injections

When sciatica pain is severe, conservative therapy alone can sometimes feel overwhelming. In these instances, short-term pharmaceutical management prescribed by a GP can assist in lowering inflammation to a tolerable level so you can stay mobile. Over-the-counter anti-inflammatories (NSAIDs) or specific nerve pain medications are frequently utilized.

If pain remains high and limits your ability to function or participate in physical therapy, an epidural steroid injection (ESI) may be considered. Cortisone is injected directly near the irritated nerve root to reduce localized swelling. While injections can offer good short-to-medium-term pain reduction, they are not guaranteed long-term fixes on their own. For a deeper breakdown of how injections compare to surgery and conservative care, read my full article on disc protrusions, cortisone injections, and surgical evidence.

Surgical Intervention: When Is It Necessary?

Surgery is rarely the starting point for sciatica, but it is an essential option for certain situations. A microdiscectomy is the most common surgical procedure, where the surgeon removes the portion of the herniated disc pressing against the nerve root.

Surgery is generally considered in two scenarios:

  • Emergency signs: Cauda equina syndrome, characterized by loss of bowel or bladder control, progressive leg weakness, or saddle anesthesia (numbness in the groin/buttock area), requires immediate emergency surgical evaluation.
  • Unrelenting pain: Severe, persistent leg pain that has failed to improve after 6 to 12 weeks of structured conservative care and severely impairs daily life.

It is worth noting that while surgery often brings faster pain relief in the first few months, long-term studies like the Spine Patient Outcomes Research Trial (SPORT) found that after two to four years, outcomes between surgical and non-surgical management were remarkably similar (PubMed / SPORT Trial, 2007). Patience and consistent conservative care pay off for most people.

Finding the Right Path for Your Recovery

There is no single treatment that works for everyone with sciatica. What helps one person might aggravate another. The key is starting with the least invasive, evidence-backed options first, giving your body time to heal, and escalating treatment only when necessary. If sciatica is keeping you from doing what you love in Hornsby, getting a thorough clinical assessment is the best way to map out a clear plan.

Frequently Asked Questions About Sciatica

How long does a typical sciatica flare-up take to resolve?

Most mild to moderate sciatica flare-ups improve significantly within 4 to 8 weeks with appropriate conservative care and modified activity. However, full recovery can sometimes take several months as disc material naturally reabsorbs and nerve inflammation settles.

Should I use ice or heat for sciatica pain?

As a general rule, use ice packs on your lower back during the first 48 to 72 hours of an acute flare-up to help reduce localized inflammation. Heat is usually better for subacute or chronic stiffness to relax tight lower back and gluteal muscles.

Can a chiropractor help with severe sciatica?

Yes, chiropractors are trained to assess nerve-related leg pain, differentiate between disc issues and muscular entrapment, and provide conservative interventions. If your condition requires medical escalation or imaging, your chiropractor will coordinate with your GP.

When should I seek immediate medical attention for leg pain?

You should seek emergency medical care if you experience sudden loss of bowel or bladder control, numbness around your genitals or buttocks, or sudden severe weakness like being unable to lift your foot (foot drop).
