Lumbar Disc Injury Management

Updated 10 October 2026 · Hornsby Chiropractor

Lumbar disc problems can cause lower back pain, sometimes with leg pain, tingling or weakness. This guide covers general day-to-day management before surgery. It cannot identify which structure is painful or replace advice based on an examination. If you have already had spinal surgery, follow your surgical team's rehabilitation plan rather than starting these suggestions on your own.

Warning signs: when to get urgent help

Seek emergency medical assessment for new difficulty passing urine or controlling your bladder or bowels, numbness around the genitals or bottom, or rapidly worsening leg weakness. Do not wait for a routine chiropractic appointment. Call Triple Zero (000) if you need an ambulance. These symptoms can signal serious nerve compression (healthdirect: spinal disc problems).

Back pain after a major injury, or pain accompanied by fever or feeling very unwell, also needs prompt medical assessment. Arrange an assessment if pain keeps limiting your usual activities, leg symptoms are developing, or you are not improving.

Keep moving at a manageable level

For uncomplicated back pain, gentle activity is generally preferable to spending long periods in bed. You do not need to push through every painful task. Choose a tolerable starting point, change position regularly and build activity gradually; the NHS slipped-disc guidance recommends gentle exercise as symptoms allow.

For example, try a short walk on a comfortable route rather than immediately returning to a long, hilly outing. Notice how you feel during the walk and afterwards. Shorten the next outing or ask for advice if symptoms steadily worsen. Walking pain does not by itself establish that a disc is being damaged. See our guide to lower back pain when walking for pacing and different symptom patterns.

Lumbar McKenzie extension exercises

Repeated extension movements are one exercise option, not a cure or a routine prescription for everyone with a disc problem. The McKenzie approach uses examination findings and changes in symptoms to select an individual programme. A 2023 Cochrane review found no clinically important benefit for acute or subacute non-specific lower back pain; that review does not establish the best exercise for every person with nerve-root symptoms or a diagnosed disc herniation.

Ask a qualified clinician whether an extension exercise is appropriate, how much to do and how to judge your response. More repetitions are not automatically better. Stop and seek advice if an exercise brings on spreading leg pain, new numbness or weakness, or a marked lasting increase in symptoms. More about assessment and exercise choices is explained in our disc protrusion and sciatica guide.

Driving and getting out of a chair

Adjust the seat so you can reach the wheel and pedals comfortably. A small lumbar support may help some people; use it only if it feels better. Plan breaks on longer journeys and change position before stiffness builds. If pain or weakness affects your ability to control the car safely, do not drive and seek medical advice.

After sitting, take your time standing up and use the armrest or another stable support if needed. You do not have to arch your back for a fixed period before every transfer. Pain during the transition does not prove that a healing disc has suddenly torn. Record the pattern and discuss it at an assessment; pain when standing after sitting can have several causes.

Sitting and desk work

Set up the chair, screen and keyboard so they are easy to use without sustained reaching or looking down. Alternate comfortable positions and take short movement breaks. A support or posture that helps one person may irritate another. The Australian Low Back Pain Clinical Care Standard supports an individual plan and a return to usual activities rather than relying on one intervention alone.

Anti-inflammatory medicines

Discuss pain relief with your GP or pharmacist. The choice depends on your medical history, other medicines and possible side effects, not pain intensity alone. Do not start, stop or substitute a medicine based on this article. Healthdirect's anti-inflammatory medicine guidance explains when to ask for advice.

If you have been told to avoid anti-inflammatory medicines, check before using a gel, cream or patch as well. A topical product is not automatically an appropriate substitute.

Preparing for an assessment

Bring a short description of when symptoms started, which movements change them, any leg symptoms and the medicines you take. Mention previous surgery and bring existing scan reports if you have them. An assessment helps decide what activity is suitable and whether further medical investigation or referral is needed.
