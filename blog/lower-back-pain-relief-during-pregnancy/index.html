Lower Back Pain During Pregnancy: Relief Tips

10 September 2026 · Lower back pain

By late afternoon, getting out of a chair, turning in bed or standing at the kitchen bench can feel much harder than it did earlier in pregnancy. Changes in posture, weight distribution and the way the pelvis is supported can all contribute to lower back discomfort during pregnancy (Mayo Clinic).

This article is general information only. It is not a diagnosis or individual treatment advice. Please speak with your GP, midwife, obstetrician or another qualified health professional about pain during pregnancy, particularly if it is new, severe or changing.

Work out where the pain is coming from

Pregnancy-related pain is not always centred in the lumbar spine. Pelvic girdle pain may be felt near one or both sacroiliac joints, across the buttocks, around the pubic bone or into the thighs. It can be aggravated by stairs, standing on one leg, getting out of a car or rolling over in bed (Pregnancy, Birth and Baby).

Pain travelling down a leg, especially with tingling, numbness or weakness, needs a more careful assessment because irritation of a spinal nerve can produce these symptoms (healthdirect). Do not assume that every leg ache is a disc problem. If you already have a diagnosed lumbar disc condition, our guide to practical lumbar disc management provides general information about sitting, driving and walking, but pregnancy may change which options are suitable.

Adjust the jobs that keep provoking it

Try changing position before pain builds rather than searching for one supposedly perfect posture. When sitting, place both feet on the floor and use a small cushion or rolled towel behind the lower back if that feels comfortable. Supportive, low-heeled footwear and avoiding prolonged standing may also reduce strain on the back (Mayo Clinic).

For lifting, keep the object close, bend through the knees and hips, and avoid twisting while carrying it. Ask for help with awkward loads, including toddlers, baskets and shopping bags. Mayo Clinic recommends squatting with a wide stance and lifting with the legs rather than bending from the waist during pregnancy (Mayo Clinic).

  • To get out of bed, roll onto your side, bring your legs over the edge and push up with your arms.
  • When dressing, sit down rather than balancing on one leg.
  • Split housework or shopping into shorter blocks and change tasks regularly.
  • In the car, bring the seat close enough that you are not reaching for the pedals or steering wheel.

Side sleeping with a pillow between the knees can make the pelvis and lower back more comfortable. A second pillow under the abdomen may help as pregnancy progresses (Mayo Clinic).

Keep moving, but make the dose manageable

Complete rest is rarely the first choice for ordinary pregnancy-related back discomfort. Regular physical activity during an uncomplicated pregnancy can support general health, although the type and amount should reflect your pregnancy, previous activity and medical advice (healthdirect). A Cochrane review found that land-based exercise may help reduce pregnancy-related low back pain, while the certainty and size of benefit varied across studies (Cochrane).

Walking for a short, comfortable period is a reasonable place to begin if your maternity clinician has not restricted exercise. Gentle pelvic tilts, supported squats or movement in a pool may suit some people, but an exercise should not be pushed through sharp pain. Stop exercising and seek medical advice if you develop bleeding, dizziness, chest pain, regular painful contractions, fluid leakage or unusual shortness of breath (healthdirect).

Use pain relief and hands-on care carefully

A wrapped warm pack over the sore part of the lower back may feel soothing. Keep the temperature comfortable, protect the skin and avoid anything that makes you overheated. Heat and cold are among the self-care options described for pregnancy-related back pain by Mayo Clinic (Mayo Clinic).

Do not start or stop a medicine because of a blog post. Some medicines can affect pregnancy, and healthdirect recommends checking with a doctor, pharmacist or midwife before taking prescription, over-the-counter or complementary products while pregnant (healthdirect). That includes pain relievers you may have used without concern before pregnancy.

Hands-on treatment is sometimes considered alongside exercise and practical changes, but research on manual therapies for pregnancy-related back and pelvic pain is limited and does not show that every technique suits every person (Cochrane). In a chiropractic assessment, I would first clarify the pain pattern, screen for warning signs and take account of the stage and circumstances of the pregnancy. Any proposed care should be comfortable, appropriately modified and coordinated with your maternity team where necessary.

Know when back pain needs prompt medical review

Contact your midwife, obstetrician, GP or maternity unit promptly if back pain is severe, follows a fall, is accompanied by vaginal bleeding, fever, pain when passing urine, fluid loss, abdominal tightening or contractions. Back pain with these symptoms can require assessment for causes unrelated to ordinary muscle or joint soreness (healthdirect).

Seek urgent medical care for new loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness around the genitals or bottom, or significant weakness in one or both legs. These are recognised warning signs associated with serious compression of spinal nerves (healthdirect). If symptoms are sudden or severe, call Triple Zero (000) or attend an emergency department.

Frequently asked questions

These answers are general. Your maternity team can adapt advice to your health, pregnancy and symptoms.

What is the best sleeping position for pregnancy back pain?

Side sleeping with one or both knees bent may be more comfortable, with a pillow between the knees and another under the abdomen if needed (Mayo Clinic). There is no need to force a position that increases your pain.

Can I see a chiropractor while pregnant?

Hands-on care may be considered after appropriate screening, but evidence for manual therapies during pregnancy remains limited (Cochrane). Tell the chiropractor how many weeks pregnant you are, mention any complications, and discuss care with your maternity clinician if there are medical concerns.

Will a pregnancy support belt help?

A pelvic support garment may be suggested as part of management for pelvic girdle pain, usually alongside advice about movement and daily activities (Pregnancy, Birth and Baby). Fit matters, so seek guidance from a physiotherapist or other qualified maternity health professional rather than tightening a belt as much as possible.

Can lower back pain be a sign of labour?

Back pain accompanied by regular contractions, pelvic pressure, vaginal bleeding or fluid loss needs prompt advice from your maternity unit, particularly before 37 weeks (healthdirect). If you are unsure, call your midwife or maternity hospital rather than waiting for the pattern to become obvious.
