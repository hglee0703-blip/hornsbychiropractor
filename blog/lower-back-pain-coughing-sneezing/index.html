Why Your Lower Back Hurts When You Cough or Sneeze

05 September 2026 · Lower back pain

You feel a sharp catch in your lower back just as you cough, sneeze or clear your throat. It may settle immediately, or leave an ache that hangs around. Sometimes the pain stays in the back. In other cases, it travels into the buttock or leg.

The cough is not necessarily the original cause. More often, that sudden movement briefly loads an area that is already irritated. The pattern of pain, how long it has been present and whether there are leg or nerve symptoms matter more than the cough itself.

What changes in your back during a cough or sneeze?

Coughing and sneezing involve a rapid contraction of the diaphragm, abdominal muscles and other muscles around the trunk. This creates a short burst of pressure while the spine is being braced, often with a small amount of bending or twisting (NCBI Bookshelf).

A comfortable back usually tolerates that without trouble. If a muscle, joint or spinal disc is already sensitive, the sudden load can reproduce pain. The same thing may happen while laughing, straining on the toilet, lifting or getting out of a low chair. Back pain can arise from several structures, and the painful structure often cannot be identified from symptoms alone (healthdirect).

Common pain patterns and what they may suggest

Pain confined to the lower back may occur with a strained or guarded muscle, irritation around the small joints of the spine, or non-specific lower back pain. Most episodes of lower back pain are described as non-specific because no single damaged structure can be confirmed as the cause (healthdirect). Pain may still be quite sharp even when the cause is not dangerous.

Pain that runs into the buttock or leg, particularly with tingling, numbness or weakness, raises more suspicion of nerve irritation. Sciatica describes pain along the sciatic nerve and is commonly associated with irritation or compression of a nerve root in the lower spine (healthdirect). Coughing or sneezing can aggravate pain related to a herniated disc because the brief pressure change may irritate an already sensitive nerve (Mayo Clinic).

A disc protrusion on a scan does not automatically explain the pain. Disc changes can be found in people without symptoms, so scan findings need to be considered alongside the examination and the distribution of pain (PubMed). For a closer look at disc-related leg pain and treatment choices, see this guide to disc protrusion, sciatica, injections and surgery.

Warning signs that need prompt attention

Most cough-related back pain is not an emergency, but some accompanying symptoms should not be watched at home. Seek urgent medical care if back pain comes with new loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness around the genitals or anus, or marked weakness in one or both legs. These can be signs of serious compression of the nerves at the base of the spine (healthdirect).

Arrange a timely medical assessment if the pain began after a significant fall or accident, is accompanied by fever, unexplained weight loss or feeling generally unwell, or is severe and getting worse. A history of cancer, osteoporosis, immune suppression or prolonged corticosteroid use also changes how back pain should be assessed (healthdirect). Pain higher in the flank with fever, nausea or urinary symptoms may come from the urinary system rather than the spine and also needs medical review (healthdirect).

What you can do over the next few days

Try not to repeatedly cough or sneeze as a test. When one is coming, face forwards, avoid twisting and gently brace your abdomen. If practical, place a hand on a bench or the back of a sturdy chair for support. This is a comfort strategy rather than a treatment, and it should not require forceful tightening.

Complete bed rest is generally discouraged for uncomplicated lower back pain. Staying reasonably active and returning gradually to normal tasks tends to be preferable, provided the activity does not cause a substantial or lasting increase in symptoms (healthdirect). Short walks and regular changes of position are often more manageable than one long walk or sitting for hours.

Heat may provide short-term comfort for some people, while medicine should be discussed with a pharmacist or GP because suitability depends on factors such as other medicines, pregnancy, kidney or stomach problems and cardiovascular health (healthdirect). Do not force a stretch or copy a disc exercise simply because coughing hurts. Exercises such as repeated McKenzie-style movements suit some presentations but can aggravate others; our practical lumbar disc management guide explains how walking, sitting, driving and exercise may be adjusted.

What an assessment may involve

During an assessment, I would generally want to know whether the pain remains central or travels down the leg, what positions alter it, and whether there is numbness, weakness or any change in bladder or bowel function. Examination may include observing movement, checking strength, reflexes and sensation, and using certain leg tests where nerve irritation is suspected.

Imaging is not routinely required for every new episode of lower back pain. A clinician may consider it when examination findings suggest a serious cause, when significant neurological problems are present, or when the result is likely to change management (healthdirect). A chiropractor should also refer for medical assessment or imaging rather than proceed with treatment if the history or examination raises concern.

Frequently asked questions

These brief answers cover common concerns, but persistent or worsening symptoms deserve an individual assessment.

Does pain when coughing mean I have a slipped disc?

No. Coughing can aggravate pain from several sensitive structures, including muscles and joints, as well as a disc. A herniated disc becomes more likely when back pain is accompanied by leg pain, tingling, numbness or weakness, but those symptoms still require proper assessment (Mayo Clinic).

Should I stop walking until the pain settles?

Usually not if the pain is uncomplicated and walking remains tolerable. Gentle activity and gradual return to normal movement are generally preferred to prolonged bed rest, but stop and seek advice if walking brings on increasing weakness, numbness or severe leg pain (healthdirect).

Can a chiropractor diagnose the cause?

A chiropractor can take a history, examine spinal movement and screen for signs of nerve involvement or serious illness. No examination can always identify one exact pain-producing structure, so referral to a GP or another service may be needed when findings are unclear or concerning (healthdirect).

How long should I wait before having it checked?

Seek immediate care for bladder or bowel changes, saddle-area numbness or substantial leg weakness (healthdirect). Otherwise, arrange an assessment if the pain is worsening, repeatedly shoots into the leg, limits normal activity, or is not beginning to settle.
