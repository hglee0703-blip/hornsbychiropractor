Shoulder Pain When Reaching Overhead: What Helps

23 September 2026 · Shoulder pain

Putting a plate into a high cupboard, hanging washing or pulling on a shirt can expose a shoulder problem quite quickly. The arm may move comfortably at waist height, then catch or ache as it rises.

That pain does not identify one particular injured structure. Rotator cuff tendinopathy is one possibility, but the bursa, shoulder joint, neck and several other tissues can produce similar symptoms, so a single self-test is rarely enough to make a diagnosis (Journal of Orthopaedic & Sports Physical Therapy, 2025).

Why reaching overhead may hurt

The rotator cuff is a group of muscles and tendons that helps control the upper arm during lifting and rotation. Rotator cuff problems commonly cause pain while raising or lowering the arm, reaching behind the back, or lying on the affected side (Mayo Clinic). This does not necessarily mean that a tendon is torn.

The pattern matters. Pain mainly through part of the lifting movement may behave differently from a shoulder that is stiff in nearly every direction. Marked loss of movement, particularly with increasing stiffness and night pain, can occur with frozen shoulder (Mayo Clinic). Tingling, numbness or pain running below the elbow may point towards irritation coming from the neck rather than the shoulder alone (Mayo Clinic).

What to do over the next few days

Reduce the aggravating load without completely guarding the arm. Move frequently within a tolerable range, but temporarily shorten or avoid the action that repeatedly produces sharp pain. Put commonly used items on a lower shelf, use a small step rather than stretching to the limit, and keep loads close to your body. Exercise and activity modification are central parts of conservative care for rotator cuff-related shoulder pain (Journal of Orthopaedic & Sports Physical Therapy, 2025).

For sleep, try lying on the other side with the sore arm resting on a pillow, or lie on your back with a folded towel or pillow supporting the elbow. A cold pack wrapped in a towel may feel useful after an aggravating activity, while some people prefer warmth for surrounding muscle tension. Avoid placing ice or a heat pack directly against the skin.

Over-the-counter pain medicines are not suitable for everyone. Ibuprofen and other anti-inflammatory medicines can interact with some medicines and may be unsuitable for people with certain stomach, kidney, heart or pregnancy-related concerns, so check with a pharmacist or doctor rather than assuming they are safe for you (healthdirect).

Three gentle exercises to try

These exercises are reasonable starting points if there has been no significant injury and you can still move the arm. Keep the effort modest. Mild discomfort can sometimes occur during rehabilitation, but sharp pain, rapidly increasing pain or symptoms that remain clearly worse afterwards are reasons to reduce the range or stop. Current clinical guidance supports an active rehabilitation plan matched to the person’s capacity rather than one standard routine for every shoulder (Journal of Orthopaedic & Sports Physical Therapy, 2025).

  • Supported wall slide: Place your forearms on a wall with a cloth under them. Gently slide upwards only as far as you can without shrugging hard or forcing through a catch. Start with six to ten slow repetitions.
  • External rotation press: Stand beside a wall with your elbow bent and tucked lightly against your side. Press the back of your hand into the wall without allowing the arm to move. Hold for 10 to 20 seconds at a comfortable effort and repeat three times.
  • Light band row: Secure an exercise band around a stable point. Draw your elbows back without thrusting the ribs forward or squeezing as hard as possible. Begin with one or two sets of eight to twelve controlled repetitions.

Try one exercise at a time so you can tell how the shoulder responds. If it feels similar or easier later that day and the following morning, build gradually. More repetitions are not automatically better, particularly if your work, gym training or sport is already placing a large load on the shoulder.

When the shoulder needs an assessment

Arrange an assessment if the problem is worsening, is disturbing sleep, keeps returning, or is not starting to settle with sensible changes. It is also worth being checked sooner if you have obvious weakness, substantial stiffness, persistent pins and needles, or difficulty using the arm for ordinary tasks. Shoulder assessment generally considers your history, neck movement, active and passive shoulder movement, strength and the effect of different loads rather than relying on one special test (Journal of Orthopaedic & Sports Physical Therapy, 2025).

Scans are not automatically required for every episode of overhead pain. Imaging may be considered after trauma, where a major tear or fracture is suspected, or when symptoms are not improving as expected and the result would change management (Journal of Orthopaedic & Sports Physical Therapy, 2025). A chiropractor, physiotherapist or GP should also consider whether the neck, general health or another condition is contributing.

Seek prompt medical attention after a significant fall if the shoulder looks deformed, you cannot move the arm, or pain is severe and sudden (healthdirect). Call Triple Zero if shoulder or arm discomfort occurs with chest pressure, shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, dizziness or pain spreading through the chest, jaw or back, as these can be symptoms of a heart attack (healthdirect).

Frequently asked questions

Overhead shoulder pain varies considerably, so these answers are general guides rather than rules for every person.

Should I keep reaching overhead if it hurts?

Do not repeatedly force through sharp or increasing pain. Temporarily reduce the height, weight or number of repetitions, while keeping the shoulder moving within a tolerable range. A graded exercise plan can then rebuild capacity according to your symptoms (Journal of Orthopaedic & Sports Physical Therapy, 2025).

How should I sleep with shoulder pain?

Avoid lying directly on the painful shoulder if that aggravates it. Try your other side with the sore arm supported on a pillow, or sleep on your back with support beneath the elbow. Seek an assessment if night pain is persistent or accompanied by pronounced stiffness.

Do I need an ultrasound or MRI?

Not necessarily. Many shoulder problems can initially be assessed from the history and physical examination, with imaging reserved for situations where the findings may alter treatment (Journal of Orthopaedic & Sports Physical Therapy, 2025). Trauma, marked weakness or failure to improve may make imaging more relevant.

Can a chiropractor assess overhead shoulder pain?

Yes, a chiropractor can assess shoulder and neck movement, strength and factors that aggravate the pain. Management should be based on the findings and may include load advice, exercises or referral to a GP or another practitioner where appropriate. Manual treatment, if used, should support rather than replace active rehabilitation (Journal of Orthopaedic & Sports Physical Therapy, 2025).
