Fix Upper Back Pain Between Shoulder Blades | Hornsby Chiro

If you spend your days working at a desk in Hornsby, driving through Sydney traffic, or scrolling on your phone, you probably know the feeling. That deep, nagging ache right between your shoulder blades. Some patients describe it as a dull knot that never quite loosens up. Others feel a sharp, burning sensation every time they turn their head or take a deep breath.

In my clinic, interscapular pain—pain located between the shoulder blades—is one of the most frequent complaints I see. The good news is that most cases stem from mechanical issues in the thoracic spine and surrounding musculature. That means with the right approach, it is very manageable. You do not have to just live with it.

Why Upper Back Pain Happens Between the Shoulder Blades

The area between your shoulder blades relies on a complex network of muscles, joints, and nerves. Your thoracic spine consists of 12 vertebrae connected to your rib cage. Layers of muscles, including your rhomboids, middle trapezius, and levator scapulae, work constantly to keep your shoulders stable and your upper body upright.

When you sit hunched over a keyboard or steering wheel, your upper back muscles spend hours in a stretched, strained position. Over time, these muscles fatigue. They become irritated, develop tender trigger points, and pull on the thoracic joints (Healthdirect, 2023). What starts as mild tightness can quickly turn into a chronic, uncomfortable burning sensation.

Common Causes: From Desk Slouching to Rib Joint Dysfunction

Not all upper back pain comes from poor posture alone. When patients visit my Hornsby practice, I look for several common underlying culprits:

1. Postural Muscle Strain: Sustained forward-head posture increases the effective weight of your head on the neck and upper back muscles (PubMed, 2016). The rhomboids and trapezius muscles have to work double-time just to keep you upright, leading to localized muscle fatigue and aching.

2. Costovertebral Joint Dysfunction: Each of your ribs connects to your thoracic spine at a small joint called the costovertebral joint. If one of these joints becomes irritated or restricted, it can cause sharp, localized pain between the shoulder blades that worsens when taking a deep breath or rotating your chest (StatPearls, 2023).

3. Thoracic Spine Stiffness: Lack of movement causes the joints in your upper back to lose their natural mobility. When the thoracic spine freezes up, your body compensates by overusing the neck or lower back. If you also suffer from lower back discomfort, keeping your upper back mobile is essential. Read our guide on lumbar disc injury management to see how spinal biomechanics influence the lower back.

4. Stress and Breathing Patterns: Stress causes us to hold tension in our upper body and adopt shallow chest breathing. This overuses the neck and upper back accessory breathing muscles, leading to persistent tightness across the shoulder blades.

Simple Exercises You Can Do at Home for Immediate Relief

You do not need fancy gym equipment to start easing upper back tightness. Here are three simple exercises I often suggest to patients in Hornsby. They help restore movement to the thoracic spine and relieve muscular tension.

Thoracic Extension over a Foam Roller or Towel: Sit on the floor and place a rolled-up towel or foam roller horizontally behind your mid-back. Support your head with your hands. Gently lean back over the roller, arching your upper spine. Hold for 3 to 5 seconds, then return up. Repeat 8 to 10 times across different sections of your upper back (PubMed Central, 2020). Stop immediately if you feel sharp pain.

Thread the Needle Stretch: Start on your hands and knees. Reach your right arm underneath your left arm, sliding your shoulder down toward the floor until you feel a gentle stretch across your upper back. Hold for 20 to 30 seconds while breathing deeply into your ribs. Repeat on the other side.

Doorway Chest Stretch: Tight chest muscles pull your shoulders forward, putting excess strain on the back muscles. Stand in a doorway, place your forearms on the door frame at 90-degree angles, and step forward gently until you feel a stretch across the front of your chest. Hold for 30 seconds.

Daily Habits That Prevent Thoracic Spine Stiffness

Exercises help, but fixing upper back pain permanently requires changing daily habits. Most people get this wrong: they try to hold a rigid 'perfect' posture all day. That just creates a different kind of muscular tension.

Movement is key. Your spine loves variety. Try setting a timer to stand up, stretch, or walk around every 45 minutes. Adjust your workstation so your screen sits at eye level and your elbows rest at a comfortable 90-degree angle.

Pay attention to how you carry heavy bags. Carrying a heavy backpack over one shoulder loads your thoracic spine unevenly, leading to muscular spasm between the shoulder blades. Use both straps whenever possible.

When Should You See a Healthcare Professional?

Most upper back pain resolves with targeted movement, ergonomic tweaks, and hands-on care. However, certain symptoms require prompt medical attention.

If your upper back pain is accompanied by chest tightness, shortness of breath, unexplained dizziness, nausea, or pain radiating into your jaw or left arm, seek emergency medical care immediately (Better Health Channel, 2022). These can be signs of a cardiovascular issue.

You should also consult a qualified chiropractor, physiotherapist, or doctor if your pain persists for more than two weeks, worsens progressively, or wakes you up in the middle of the night. A thorough physical assessment helps pinpoint whether your pain stems from joint restriction, rib subluxation, or muscle tears.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does upper back pain between the shoulder blades take to heal?

Most mild muscle strains or postural irritations improve within 1 to 3 weeks with light exercise, ergonomic changes, and conservative care. If joint restriction or chronic muscular compensation is present, full recovery may take 4 to 6 weeks under a tailored rehabilitation plan.

Can upper back pain between shoulder blades be a sign of something serious?

While most upper back pain is musculoskeletal, sudden severe pain accompanied by chest pressure, shortness of breath, or pain radiating to the jaw or arm can signal a cardiac event (Better Health Channel, 2022). Seek urgent medical care if these red flag symptoms occur.

Is massage or chiropractic better for interscapular pain?

Both modalities offer great benefits. Massage focuses primarily on relieving soft tissue tension and trigger points in overworked muscles like the rhomboids. Chiropractic care addresses underlying joint restrictions in the thoracic spine and rib cage, addressing the root biomechanical cause.

Can posture correctors help with pain between shoulder blades?

Posture correctors can offer temporary awareness, but relying on them long-term can actually weaken your postural muscles. It is far better to build active back strength and thoracic mobility through daily exercises rather than relying on a brace.
