Relieve Neck and Shoulder Pain After Freestyle Swimming

01 November 2026 · Andy Lee, Chiropractor · Neck & shoulder pain

You climb out of the pool after a freestyle session and notice a tight ache running from the side of your neck into the top of one shoulder. It may settle quickly, or become more obvious later that day. If you are trying to relieve neck and shoulder pain after freestyle swimming, start by reducing the irritating load rather than forcing another hard session through it.

Freestyle involves repeated overhead arm movements, shoulder-blade control and rotation through the upper body. Fatigue, a sudden increase in swimming volume or an inefficient stroke can place extra demand on the shoulder and surrounding muscles (NCBI Bookshelf). Neck discomfort may also come from muscles, joints or irritated nerves, so not every post-swim ache has the same cause (healthdirect).

Why freestyle can make the area sore

Your neck should stay relatively quiet while your body rolls to breathe. If you lift or twist your head sharply for each breath, the muscles along one side of the neck may work harder than necessary. Breathing only to one side is not automatically harmful, but a marked difference between sides can be useful information for a swimming coach or clinician.

The shoulder has its own workload. Each stroke asks the arm to recover overhead while the shoulder blade moves on the rib cage. Repetition, fatigue and changes in shoulder mechanics are recognised contributors to swimming-related shoulder pain (NCBI Bookshelf). The answer is usually not one perfect posture. It is more useful to look at technique, training volume, recovery and how comfortably the neck and shoulder move on land.

A practical plan for neck and shoulder pain after freestyle swimming

For the next day or two, avoid the movement that clearly reproduces the pain. That may mean skipping a hard swim, reducing the distance or using an easy kick set without gripping the board tightly. Complete bed rest is rarely necessary for uncomplicated neck pain, and gentle activity is commonly advised as comfort allows (healthdirect).

Try slow neck turns and shoulder rolls within an easy range. Do not push into a pinch, sharp pain or tingling. A wrapped cold pack or gentle warmth may be used for comfort, with a cloth between the pack and your skin; healthdirect includes heat and cold among common self-care options for neck pain (healthdirect).

Check what changed before the symptoms began. A longer session, paddles, faster intervals, less recovery or a return after time away can all raise the workload. When the area settles, build back gradually instead of testing it with the same demanding session straight away.

Adjust your stroke before adding more laps

On your next easy swim, think about turning your chest and head together to breathe. Keep one goggle near the water rather than lifting your whole face. Exhale into the water so the breath itself does not become rushed. These are cues, not rigid rules, and a qualified swim coach watching from the side and front can see details that are hard to feel while swimming.

Notice where your hand enters. Reaching across the centre line can make the upper body wobble, while an entry that is excessively wide may feel awkward at the shoulder. Aim for a relaxed entry roughly in front of that shoulder, then let the shoulder blade move rather than holding it down and back throughout the stroke.

Equipment matters too. Hand paddles increase the resistance against the arm, so they may not suit an irritable shoulder. A kickboard can also be uncomfortable if you hold it with straight arms and keep your head lifted for a long time. Remove the aggravating tool first, then judge the basic stroke on its own.

Simple movements to practise on land

Begin with a small chin nod, as though making the back of your neck gently longer. Hold without clenching your jaw, then relax. Follow it with slow left and right neck rotations. Five comfortable repetitions are enough to learn how the area responds; more is not necessarily better when it is freshly irritated.

For the shoulder blades, stand with your arms relaxed and lightly draw each shoulder blade towards the back pocket on the same side. Avoid a forceful military posture. You can then try a gentle wall slide, moving your forearms upwards only as far as you can without shrugging, pinching or increasing the pain.

Exercise can help some forms of persistent mechanical neck pain, although the appropriate exercise and expected response vary between people (Cochrane). Swimming-specific pain may need a different plan from discomfort caused by desk work or a recent injury. When an assessment is sensible

Arrange an assessment if the pain keeps returning whenever you swim, is getting worse, disrupts sleep or has not begun to settle with a short period of modified activity. Weakness, persistent pins and needles, numbness or pain travelling down the arm also deserves clinical attention because neck symptoms can sometimes involve a nerve (healthdirect).

Seek urgent medical help after a significant collision or dive injury, or if neck pain occurs with severe headache, fever, loss of balance, new weakness or loss of bladder or bowel control. These can be warning signs requiring prompt medical assessment (Mayo Clinic). Call 000 for severe shoulder or neck discomfort accompanied by chest pressure, breathing difficulty, sweating or nausea.

During a musculoskeletal appointment, I would generally examine neck and shoulder movement, strength, neurological signs where relevant, and the training changes around the onset. Care may involve advice, graded exercise and hands-on treatment where appropriate, rather than simply telling you to stop swimming. Frequently asked questions

Can I keep swimming with mild neck or shoulder soreness?

You may be able to continue with an easier, shorter session if the discomfort remains mild, does not alter your stroke and settles afterwards. Stop if pain becomes sharp, spreads down the arm or steadily worsens. Gentle activity is generally preferred to prolonged inactivity for uncomplicated neck pain (healthdirect).

Should I stretch the sore shoulder before swimming?

Gentle movement may feel useful, but forceful stretching into pain is not a good warm-up. Start with easy shoulder and upper-body movements, then use relaxed laps to prepare for harder swimming. If one particular stretch repeatedly causes a pinch or arm symptoms, leave it out until the area has been assessed.

Why does it hurt only when I breathe to one side?

One-sided pain can reflect how far you turn your head, how your body rolls or how that shoulder manages the stroke. It does not identify one diagnosis by itself. A coach can assess the stroke, while a clinician can check movement, strength and possible nerve-related symptoms.

How soon can I return to normal training?

Return once everyday movement and easy swimming are comfortable, then increase one training variable at a time. Begin with distance or pace before reintroducing paddles and demanding intervals. If each attempt causes the same pain, further assessment is more useful than repeatedly resting and retesting.
