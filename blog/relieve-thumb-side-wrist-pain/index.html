Relieve Thumb-Side Wrist Pain While Holding a Baby

10 November 2026 · Andy Lee, Chiropractor · Wrist & hand pain

You lift your baby from the cot with your thumb spread wide and your wrist bent. A sharp ache catches at the base of the thumb, then lingers while feeding, settling or fastening the capsule. If you are trying to relieve thumb-side wrist pain, the first useful step is to reduce that repeated combination of gripping, thumb extension and a bent wrist.

This pattern is often associated with de Quervain’s tenosynovitis, sometimes called “mummy thumb” or “parent’s wrist”. It involves irritation around two thumb tendons where they pass through a narrow tunnel near the wrist, and caring for a baby can repeatedly load this area (NCBI Bookshelf).

What may be causing the pain

De Quervain’s pain usually sits on the thumb side of the wrist and may spread a short way into the thumb or forearm. Gripping, lifting, twisting a lid or moving the thumb can aggravate it, and there may be local tenderness or swelling (Mayo Clinic).

Not every sore wrist is de Quervain’s. A joint sprain, thumb-base arthritis, another tendon problem, nerve irritation or an injury to one of the wrist bones can produce overlapping symptoms (NCBI Bookshelf). Avoid repeatedly provoking the wrist with online self-tests. A clinician can usually assess the pattern from the history and examination, with imaging considered when the diagnosis is uncertain or another injury is suspected (NCBI Bookshelf).

How to relieve thumb-side wrist pain while lifting

Start by changing the position that hurts most. Rather than hooking your hands under the baby’s armpits with each thumb pointing upwards, slide your palms and forearms under the baby, keep your thumbs more relaxed and bring the baby close before standing. Try to keep the wrist in line with the forearm instead of letting it sag towards the little-finger side. Activity modification and avoiding repetitive aggravating movements are commonly recommended parts of early management (Mayo Clinic).

  • From the cot: move close to the rail, bend through your knees and hips, and gather the baby towards your chest before straightening.
  • From the floor: kneel beside the baby, support them with both forearms, then rise while keeping the load close.
  • With the capsule: use both hands where practical and avoid carrying it at arm’s length.
  • During settling: swap sides regularly rather than keeping the same wrist cocked under the baby for a long period.

These are load-sharing ideas, not rigid rules. The baby must stay securely supported. If changing your grip feels awkward or unsafe, place the baby down safely and reset your position rather than adjusting mid-lift.

Make feeding, pram and nappy changes easier

During a feed, build the support up to the baby rather than holding the full weight with one hand. A firm pillow on your lap can support the baby’s body while your forearm supports the head and neck. Keep your hand relatively open, with the wrist resting rather than hovering in a bent position. The same principle applies while bottle feeding.

Pram buckles, clothing press-studs and nappy tabs can be surprisingly irritating. Use the larger joints where possible: press with the heel of your hand, use two hands for stiff clips, and turn your whole forearm rather than repeatedly twisting through the thumb. If computer or phone use is adding to the problem, these suggestions for wrist and forearm pain from mouse use may also help.

Short, frequent pauses are usually more realistic than complete rest with a baby. Set up commonly used items between waist and chest height, and place feeding supplies within easy reach before you sit down. This reduces the number of awkward one-handed reaches while the other arm is occupied.

Bracing, cold packs and gentle movement

A thumb-spica brace supports both the wrist and the base of the thumb. That is different from a simple wrist band, which may leave the painful thumb tendons working. Research comparing treatments suggests that thumb-spica immobilisation can form part of care, particularly alongside a clinician-administered corticosteroid injection, although the right treatment depends on the person and the diagnosis (PubMed).

A brace should feel supportive rather than tight. Remove it if it causes numbness, colour change, increasing pain or skin irritation, and ask a pharmacist, GP or treating clinician to check the fit. A wrapped cold pack may be used for a short period after an aggravating task, while avoiding direct contact between ice and skin (Mayo Clinic). If you are breastfeeding or have other health conditions, check with a pharmacist or GP before taking anti-inflammatory or pain-relieving medicine.

Once the wrist is less irritable, gentle movement can help prevent unnecessary stiffness. With your forearm supported, slowly move the thumb towards and away from the palm, staying within a comfortable range. You can also move the wrist gently while keeping the fingers relaxed. Do not force a stretch into sharp pain. Rehabilitation may include progressive exercise, but the timing and amount should be matched to symptoms and the working diagnosis (NCBI Bookshelf).

When the wrist needs an assessment

Arrange an assessment if the pain is worsening, keeps returning, affects your grip or is not beginning to settle after modifying the aggravating tasks. Seek more urgent medical care after a significant fall or impact, or if there is obvious deformity, marked swelling, loss of movement, persistent numbness, a pale or cold hand, fever, or a red and hot wrist. These features can point to an injury or illness that needs prompt investigation.

At a wrist assessment, I would look at the exact location of the pain, thumb and wrist movement, grip, tendon loading and whether symptoms might be coming from another structure. If the findings suggest a fracture, infection or another condition outside conservative musculoskeletal care, referral for medical review or imaging is appropriate.

For persistent de Quervain’s symptoms, a GP or hand specialist may discuss corticosteroid injection. Surgery is generally reserved for symptoms that do not respond to suitable non-surgical care (NCBI Bookshelf). Frequently asked questions

Can I keep lifting my baby with a sore wrist?

Often you can continue necessary care while changing your grip, sharing the load and avoiding the positions that clearly aggravate the wrist. Complete rest is rarely practical, but repeated painful loading can keep the tendon area irritated, so seek an assessment if lifting is becoming difficult or unsafe (NCBI Bookshelf).

Should I wear a thumb brace all day?

Not automatically. The useful type and wearing schedule depend on the diagnosis, symptom severity, skin comfort and the tasks you need to perform. A clinician may recommend a thumb-spica brace for selected activities or periods of rest rather than continuous unsupervised use (PubMed).

Does breastfeeding cause this wrist pain?

Breastfeeding itself does not confirm the diagnosis. The sustained wrist position used during breast or bottle feeding, combined with frequent lifting and settling, can aggravate a sensitive thumb-tendon area. De Quervain’s is recognised during pregnancy and the post-partum period, but an examination is still needed to distinguish it from other causes of wrist pain (NCBI Bookshelf).

How long does de Quervain’s wrist pain last?

There is no single timetable. Recovery depends on how irritable the area is, how often it is loaded and whether another wrist problem is present. Symptoms that persist or worsen despite sensible activity changes should be assessed rather than repeatedly pushed through (Mayo Clinic).
