Put On Socks With Sciatica: Shoes Without the Strain

19 November 2026 · Andy Lee, Chiropractor · Lower back pain

You are sitting on the edge of the bed with a sock in your hand, but your foot feels a long way away. If you are trying to put on socks with sciatica, the usual forward bend can bring on leg pain, tingling or a sharp pull through the back of the thigh. Sciatica commonly affects the path from the lower back into one leg and may include pain, numbness or weakness (Mayo Clinic).

The aim is not to force a textbook posture. It is to find a steady position that lets you dress without repeatedly provoking your symptoms or risking a fall. The options below can also help during a flare of disc-related lower back pain.

Why socks and shoes can feel so awkward

Reaching your feet usually involves bending the lower back, lifting a leg and sometimes twisting at the same time. If one of those movements is sensitive, combining them can be particularly uncomfortable. A herniated lumbar disc is one possible cause of irritation around a spinal nerve, although sciatica can have other causes too (Mayo Clinic).

The difficult movement is not identical for everyone. Some people dislike rounding forward, while others are more troubled by lifting the knee or turning the hip out. Notice which part of the task starts the leg symptoms. Our article about lower back pain while bending forward discusses this pattern in more detail.

How to put on socks with sciatica

Start with a firm chair rather than standing or perching on a soft mattress. Your hips should be supported, both feet should initially be on the floor, and the sock should already be opened up so you are not struggling with it at full reach.

  • Try the ankle-over-knee position. Bring one ankle towards the opposite knee while keeping your back reasonably comfortable. Move the leg with your hands if needed. Do not push the knee down or force the hip open.
  • Bring the foot closer, rather than chasing it. Slide the sock over the toes first, then draw it over the heel with small hand movements. Sit back between stages if symptoms begin to build.
  • Use a low footstool. If crossing the ankle is uncomfortable, place the foot on a stable step in front of you. Hinge forward only as far as feels manageable, keeping one hand on the chair for support.
  • Dress the more difficult side first. This is often easier while your energy and concentration are fresh. It also avoids leaving the hardest task until you are rushing.

Stop if the position produces increasing pain down the leg, spreading pins and needles or a sense that the leg may give way. A mild back ache and clear nerve-type symptoms are not necessarily the same experience, so use the leg response as your guide rather than trying to stretch further.

A steadier sequence for putting on shoes

Place the shoes beside the chair before you sit down. Loosen the laces or straps fully, then use the same ankle-over-knee or footstool position that worked for the sock. Trying to hold a shoe open while balancing on one leg adds difficulty that you do not need.

  • Guide the toes into the shoe while seated.
  • Set the foot down and gently press the heel in, rather than stamping.
  • Use a long-handled shoehorn if reaching behind the heel causes you to twist.
  • Bring the foot back towards you to fasten the shoe, or use elastic laces if tying a bow is the troublesome part.

Avoid standing with your foot on a chair unless you are confident in your balance and have secure support. If shoes must be put on near the front door, a stable chair with a backrest is more useful than a low stool that makes you fold deeply.

Dressing aids and footwear changes

A sock aid can be useful when neither crossing the leg nor using a footstool is comfortable. The sock is loaded onto a shaped sleeve, lowered to the floor and pulled onto the foot using long cords. An occupational therapist or medical supply shop can help if hand weakness, hip stiffness or another condition makes the standard design difficult to use.

Long-handled shoehorns, elastic laces and shoes with a wide opening can also reduce the amount of reaching. Slip-on footwear may be convenient, but it still needs to fit securely. A loose heel or smooth sole can create a different problem if your leg already feels unreliable.

These tools are best treated as temporary or ongoing practical help, not proof that you must avoid bending forever. General guidance for back pain encourages staying active within tolerable limits rather than prolonged bed rest (healthdirect). For a broader discussion of pacing and movement, see lumbar disc injury management.

When to stop experimenting and get assessed

Arrange an assessment if dressing remains difficult, your leg symptoms are worsening, or weakness is affecting walking and balance. A clinician can check strength, reflexes, sensation and movement, then consider whether further investigation is appropriate. Seek urgent medical help for new loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness around the genitals or buttocks, or rapidly worsening leg weakness. These can be warning signs of serious nerve compression and should not be managed by changing your sock technique (healthdirect). If the situation is an emergency in Australia, call Triple Zero on 000 (Triple Zero).

Until you have been assessed, keep the task simple. Sit down, prepare everything within reach and use the method that causes the least spread of symptoms into the leg. There is no prize for getting dressed the usual way during a painful flare.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to sit or stand when putting on socks?

Sitting on a firm, stable chair is usually the steadier option because it removes the need to balance on one leg. Keep the floor clear and avoid a chair with wheels. If sitting itself is uncomfortable, a sock aid may let you stay more upright.

Should I stretch through the pain to reach my foot?

Do not force a position that makes pain, tingling or numbness travel further down the leg. Back off, change the height of your foot support or use a long-handled aid. Persistent or worsening neurological symptoms warrant clinical assessment (Mayo Clinic).

Do I need a scan because dressing hurts?

Difficulty reaching a sock does not, by itself, establish the cause or mean that imaging is required. Assessment usually begins with the history and a physical examination, while scans are considered according to the symptoms and clinical findings (healthdirect). Seek prompt care if weakness, bladder or bowel changes, or saddle numbness develops.

What if I cannot cross my ankle over my knee?

Use a low, stable footstool or a sock aid instead. Do not force the hip outward, particularly if you have hip pain, a recent operation or specific movement restrictions. Follow any instructions already given by your surgeon or treating practitioner.
