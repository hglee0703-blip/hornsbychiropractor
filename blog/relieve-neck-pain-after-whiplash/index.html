Neck Pain After Whiplash: What Helps

20 September 2026 · Neck & posture

You wake the morning after a rear-end collision and your neck feels much worse than it did at the scene. Turning to check traffic is awkward, your shoulders are tight and a headache may be starting. Whiplash symptoms can appear several hours after an incident and may include neck pain, stiffness, headache, dizziness or pain around the shoulders and arms (Mayo Clinic).

The steps below are intended for uncomplicated whiplash. A significant collision, worsening symptoms or any sign of nerve or spinal injury needs medical assessment rather than home care alone.

First, check for signs that need urgent care

Call 000 or attend an emergency department if neck pain follows a serious crash and you have weakness or numbness in an arm or leg, difficulty walking, loss of bladder or bowel control, trouble breathing, confusion or loss of consciousness. Severe pain after trauma also warrants prompt medical attention (healthdirect).

Arrange an early appointment with a GP or other qualified health professional if pain is spreading down an arm, you have persistent pins and needles, your headache is severe or unusual, or the symptoms keep worsening. A clinician can examine your movement, strength, reflexes and sensation to decide whether further investigation is needed (Mayo Clinic).

If the crash was recent, write down the symptoms you noticed and when they began. Bring details of any medication, previous neck problems and the collision itself to your appointment.

Settle the pain without keeping the neck still

A wrapped cold pack may ease soreness during the first day or two, while gentle warmth can be more comfortable for muscle tightness later on. Either option should be used for short periods with a cloth protecting the skin (Mayo Clinic). Choose the one that feels soothing rather than forcing yourself to use heat or ice.

Paracetamol or an anti-inflammatory medicine may be suitable for some adults, but the right choice depends on your health, other medicines and pregnancy status. Anti-inflammatory medicines such as ibuprofen can cause side effects and are not appropriate for everyone, so check with a pharmacist or GP and follow the packet directions (healthdirect).

Avoid spending the day holding your head rigid or lying down because movement feels uncomfortable. Current clinical guidance favours advice, gradual activity and neck exercise over prolonged rest for most uncomplicated whiplash injuries (PubMed). A soft collar is not routinely recommended because extended use may reduce muscle activity and delay recovery (Mayo Clinic).

Begin with small, comfortable movements

Start with ordinary movement rather than a hard stretch. While sitting upright, slowly turn your head a little to each side, then look gently up and down. Small side-bending movements and relaxed shoulder rolls may also be used if they do not bring on sharp pain, dizziness or arm symptoms. Range-of-motion and strengthening exercises are commonly included in whiplash rehabilitation (Mayo Clinic).

Do a few comfortable repetitions at several points through the day rather than one forceful session. Mild stiffness can be expected, but stop and seek advice if an exercise produces increasing pain, numbness, weakness, marked dizziness or pain travelling further down the arm. Exercise should be adjusted to the individual after assessment when symptoms are more than mild (PubMed).

Keep walking and return to normal household tasks in manageable amounts. At a desk, change position regularly and bring the screen to a height that allows your head to rest comfortably over your shoulders. For sleep, use a pillow height that keeps the neck in a comfortable position and avoid any posture that clearly aggravates symptoms. Staying active within tolerable limits is generally preferred to prolonged inactivity for whiplash-associated neck pain (Better Health Channel).

Know when an assessment may change the plan

Most whiplash injuries do not automatically require an X-ray, CT scan or MRI. Imaging is selected according to the details of the collision, examination findings and concern about fracture, dislocation or injury affecting nerves or the spinal cord (Mayo Clinic). A scan is not a substitute for a careful history and physical examination.

For persistent pain, treatment may combine education, progressive exercise and hands-on care such as mobilisation where appropriate. Clinical guidelines support a multimodal approach rather than relying on one passive treatment by itself (PubMed). High-force neck treatment is not something to begin without first screening for fracture, neurological problems and other reasons it may be unsuitable.

Driving should wait until you can comfortably look far enough in both directions to check traffic and blind spots. Do not drive if pain or dizziness affects control of the vehicle, or if a medicine makes you drowsy. Some prescription and over-the-counter medicines can impair driving ability, and their warnings should be followed (healthdirect).

Frequently asked questions

These answers cover common concerns, but they cannot account for the severity of a particular collision or your individual health.

How long does neck pain after whiplash last?

Many people improve within several weeks, although symptoms can last longer and recovery varies from person to person (Mayo Clinic). Seek a review if you are not gradually improving, daily activities remain difficult or new symptoms appear.

Do I need an X-ray after whiplash?

Not necessarily. A clinician uses the circumstances of the injury and examination findings to decide whether imaging is needed; X-rays and scans are mainly used when there is concern about bone injury or another significant problem (Mayo Clinic).

Should I wear a neck collar?

Soft collars are generally not advised for routine, prolonged use after uncomplicated whiplash because keeping the neck still can weaken supporting muscles and interfere with recovery (Mayo Clinic). A collar may occasionally be prescribed for a specific injury, so follow the advice of the clinician who examined you.

Can massage or chiropractic care help?

Hands-on care may be considered as one part of treatment, alongside advice and progressive exercise, rather than as the whole plan. Guidelines support combining appropriate manual therapy with active care for selected people with neck pain and whiplash-associated disorders (PubMed). An examination should come first, particularly after a substantial collision or where neurological symptoms are present.
