How to Relieve Tailbone Pain from Sitting

06 September 2026 · Lower back pain

You finish a long day at the desk, stand up, and feel a sharp or bruised ache right at the bottom of your spine. The chair may be comfortable for the rest of your back, yet sitting through another meeting feels increasingly unpleasant. Pain in this area is often called coccyx pain or coccydynia, and it commonly becomes worse while sitting or as you rise from a chair (Ochsner Journal, 2014).

The aim is not to find one perfect posture and hold it all day. It is to reduce direct pressure on the sore spot, change position regularly and give irritated tissues a chance to settle.

Why sitting can aggravate the tailbone

The coccyx is the small section of bone at the base of the spine, with several muscles and ligaments attaching around it. Sitting, particularly while leaning backwards, can place pressure through this area (StatPearls: Coccyx Pain).

A fall onto the bottom is one possible cause, but symptoms may also develop after repeated or prolonged sitting. Childbirth, altered coccyx mobility and referred pain from nearby structures are among the other recognised contributors (BJA Education, 2021).

Typical coccyx pain is quite local. You can often point to it with one finger, and sitting or moving from sitting to standing may reproduce it (Ochsner Journal, 2014). Pain spreading down a leg, numbness or weakness is less typical and deserves a broader lower back and neurological assessment. If those are your main symptoms, this guide to practical lumbar disc management may be more relevant.

Adjust your chair and take pressure off the sore area

Start with the simplest change: avoid sitting directly on the painful point. A coccyx cushion usually has a rear cut-out or wedge shape so the tailbone sits over the gap rather than being compressed against the seat. Modified cushions and ergonomic changes are commonly included in conservative care, although research has not established one ideal cushion for everyone (StatPearls: Coccyx Pain).

  • Place the cushion the right way around. The cut-out belongs at the back, beneath the coccyx.
  • Keep both feet supported. Adjust the chair or use a footrest rather than perching with your legs dangling.
  • Sit back through your thighs and sitting bones. Try not to slump and roll onto the tailbone.
  • Test small changes. A cushion that pushes you into an awkward position is not helping, even if it is marketed for coccyx pain.

A slight forward lean from the hips can unload the coccyx, provided you are not rounding and straining the rest of your back. If possible, alternate between a supportive chair, standing work and short periods away from the desk. There is no clinically proven break interval that suits every person, so use symptoms as a prompt rather than waiting until the area is very sore.

Keep moving without repeatedly provoking it

Complete rest is rarely practical and can leave the rest of your back feeling stiff. General back pain guidance favours continuing comfortable daily activity rather than prolonged bed rest (healthdirect). A brief walk, standing phone call or gentle change of position may be easier than trying to stretch the painful area while seated.

For the next few days, reduce activities that put direct or repeated pressure on the tailbone. That may include long drives, cycling, rowing or sitting on a hard bench. When driving, set the seat so you can reach the pedals without sliding your pelvis forwards, and stop for movement breaks on longer trips.

Heat or a wrapped cold pack may provide temporary comfort, and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory medicines are sometimes used as part of initial conservative treatment for coccydynia (StatPearls: Coccyx Pain). Anti-inflammatory medicines are not suitable for everyone and can cause significant side effects, so check with a pharmacist or doctor if you have other health conditions, take regular medicines, are pregnant or are unsure (healthdirect). Do not put ice directly against the skin.

When tailbone pain needs an assessment

Arrange an assessment if the pain is persistent, is getting worse, repeatedly returns, or is stopping you from working, sleeping or managing normal activities. A clinician may examine the lower back, pelvis and coccyx region while also checking for other sources of pain. Imaging is not automatically needed for every sore tailbone, but may be considered where the history or examination suggests trauma, abnormal movement or another condition (BJA Education, 2021).

Seek prompt medical care after a significant fall, or if tailbone pain occurs with fever, unexplained weight loss, a growing lump, skin redness or drainage. New leg weakness, numbness around the groin or loss of bladder or bowel control requires urgent assessment because these can be warning signs of serious spinal nerve compression (healthdirect).

Treatment should match the likely cause. Conservative management can include activity changes, cushions, appropriate exercise and, in selected cases, hands-on care or pelvic floor physiotherapy. Evidence for manual treatment of coccydynia is limited, while injections and surgery are generally reserved for persistent cases assessed by an appropriate medical specialist (Ochsner Journal, 2014). As a chiropractor, I would not treat a sore tailbone based on its location alone. The pattern of symptoms and examination findings matter.

Frequently asked questions

How long does tailbone pain from sitting last?

Recovery time varies with the cause, how irritated the area is and whether it continues to be compressed. Many cases are managed conservatively, but persistent or worsening pain should be assessed rather than simply tolerated (StatPearls: Coccyx Pain).

Is a doughnut cushion good for tailbone pain?

It may help some people, but a rear cut-out coccyx cushion often targets the painful area more directly. Choose the option that unloads the tailbone without creating pressure elsewhere, and stop using it if symptoms become worse (BJA Education, 2021).

Should I exercise with a sore tailbone?

Comfortable walking and ordinary movement are usually reasonable if they do not increase the pain. Temporarily reduce exercise involving direct seat pressure, then return gradually as symptoms allow. Seek advice if movement causes severe pain, weakness or numbness (healthdirect).

Can a chiropractor help with coccyx pain?

A chiropractor can assess whether the symptoms appear to come from the coccyx, lower back or another nearby structure. Hands-on treatment is not suitable for every cause, and the evidence for manual treatment of coccydynia remains limited. Referral for medical assessment or imaging may be more appropriate after trauma, with red flags or when pain does not improve (Ochsner Journal, 2014).
