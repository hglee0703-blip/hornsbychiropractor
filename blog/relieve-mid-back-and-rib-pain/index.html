Relieve Mid-Back and Rib Pain When Twisting

13 October 2026 · Andy Lee, Chiropractor · Mid-back & rib pain

You turn to reverse the car, reach behind you in bed, or take a full breath and feel a sharp catch beside the shoulder blade. If you are trying to relieve mid-back and rib pain, start by easing the movements that clearly provoke it while checking for symptoms that do not fit a simple muscular or joint problem. Pain linked to breathing deserves a little care because it can come from the chest wall, ribs or thoracic spine, but it can also occur with conditions affecting the lungs or heart (healthdirect).

The aim is not to force the area loose. For the first day or two, use comfortable movement, avoid repeatedly testing the painful twist, and watch how the symptoms behave. A changing or worsening pattern matters more than finding the perfect stretch.

What pain with twisting or breathing may mean

A local strain can follow lifting, coughing, sport, an awkward sleep or a sudden turn. The small joints of the mid-back and the tissues around the ribs may also become sore with movement. Pain arising from the chest wall is often affected by body position, breathing or pressure over the tender area, although these features cannot confirm the cause by themselves (Mayo Clinic).

Notice whether the discomfort is sharp, aching or burning, and whether it wraps around one side. Check for recent illness, a persistent cough, a fall or a direct blow. Rib injuries commonly hurt with deep breathing, coughing and movement, and significant trauma may require medical assessment rather than home treatment alone (healthdirect).

Pain that reliably changes with twisting may suggest a musculoskeletal component, but it does not rule out another cause. Pleurisy, for example, can produce sharp chest pain that becomes worse during breathing or coughing (Mayo Clinic).

How to relieve mid-back and rib pain at home

Keep ordinary activity light but do not stay completely still for long periods. Short walks around the house or office and regular changes of position are usually more practical than spending the whole day in bed. General back-pain guidance supports remaining active as tolerated and modifying tasks that aggravate symptoms (healthdirect).

A wrapped heat pack may settle muscle guarding, while some people prefer a wrapped cold pack after a recent strain. Protect your skin, keep the temperature comfortable and stop if it increases the pain. If you are considering paracetamol or an anti-inflammatory medicine, follow the packet directions and ask a pharmacist or GP whether it is suitable for you, particularly if you take other medicines or have existing health conditions (healthdirect).

Try not to hold your breath all day because breathing hurts. Take quiet, relaxed breaths within a tolerable range, then occasionally allow a slightly fuller breath without forcing through a sharp catch. If pain prevents normal breathing, or you become breathless rather than simply sore, seek medical help promptly (healthdirect).

Gentle movements worth trying

These are small mobility drills, not tests of flexibility. Choose one or two and practise slowly once or twice through the day. Stop if pain becomes sharper, spreads into the chest or arm, or brings on dizziness or breathlessness.

  • Supported seated turn: Sit upright with both feet down. Cross your arms loosely and turn a small distance towards the comfortable side, then return to the middle. Explore the sore direction only as far as it remains easy.
  • Side-lying rotation: Lie on the more comfortable side with your knees bent and a pillow under your head. Reach the top arm forward, then slowly open it towards the other side while allowing your upper trunk to follow. Keep the range small if a deep breath catches.
  • Wall-supported reach: Place both hands on a wall at shoulder height and step back slightly. Let your chest move gently away from the wall as you breathe out, then return. This should feel mild, not like a strong stretch across the ribs.

Avoid aggressive twisting, repeated self-cracking or heavy rowing and pressing while each repetition still causes a sharp jab. As the area settles, build normal movement back gradually rather than jumping straight into the activity that triggered it. If desk work appears to be keeping the area stiff, review your chair, screen and movement breaks using this practical guide to standing desk and workstation setup.

When breathing-related pain needs medical care

Call Triple Zero on 000 for severe or unexplained chest pain, especially with chest pressure or tightness, sweating, nausea, collapse, marked shortness of breath, or pain spreading to the jaw, neck, back or arm. Australian guidance treats these symptoms as a possible medical emergency (healthdirect).

Seek urgent assessment for sudden chest or upper-back pain with unexplained breathlessness, coughing up blood, faintness or a very fast heartbeat. These can occur with a pulmonary embolism and should not be managed by stretching or waiting for a chiropractic appointment (healthdirect). Fever, a new cough, feeling markedly unwell, or pain that is steadily worsening also warrants medical advice.

Arrange an assessment after a fall or blow to the ribs, particularly if breathing is becoming harder. You should also speak with a GP if the pain is persistent, keeps returning, wakes you regardless of position, or is accompanied by weakness, numbness, an unexplained rash or unexplained weight loss. Healthdirect is available on 1800 022 222 if you are unsure how urgently you need care.

What an appointment may involve

An assessment should begin with the onset and location of the pain, recent injury or illness, breathing symptoms, and relevant medical history. Examination may include observing breathing, checking comfortable trunk movement, and assessing the spine, ribs and nearby muscles. If the pattern suggests a condition outside musculoskeletal care, referral to a GP or emergency service takes priority.

For a presentation that appears mechanical, management may include advice, graded exercises and, where suitable, gentle manual treatment. The choice depends on your examination, preferences and tolerance rather than using the same technique for every sore rib. Frequently asked questions

What is the best sleeping position for sore ribs?

Use the position that lets you breathe and turn with the least discomfort. Side sleeping on the more comfortable side with a supportive pillow, or lying on your back with the upper body slightly raised, may be easier. Avoid a position that causes increasing breathlessness or severe chest pain.

Does sharp pain mean a rib is out of place?

No. A sharp catch does not establish that a rib has moved out of place. Muscles, joints and irritated chest-wall tissues can all produce pain during movement or breathing, and examination may be needed to distinguish these from non-musculoskeletal causes (Mayo Clinic).

Should I stretch through the pain?

Do not force through sharp, escalating or breath-stopping pain. Use a smaller range that feels controlled, then reassess later. Sudden worsening, breathlessness or chest pressure is a reason to stop and seek medical advice.

Can a chiropractor help with rib pain?

Chiropractic care may be considered when assessment indicates that the symptoms are musculoskeletal and no warning signs are present. Care might involve movement advice, exercise or gentle manual treatment, but suitability depends on the cause and your health history. Chest or breathing symptoms that suggest a medical condition require referral rather than spinal treatment.
