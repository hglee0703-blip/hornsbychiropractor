Neck Pain and Stiffness After a Long Drive

16 September 2026 · Neck & posture

You pull into the driveway after several hours on the road, turn your head to reverse, and notice that your neck does not want to move. The discomfort may sit across the shoulders, at the base of the skull or mainly on one side.

Long periods in one position can contribute to neck pain and stiffness, particularly if the head is held forward or turned repeatedly. Most uncomplicated neck pain improves with simple care, although the cause and recovery time vary from person to person (healthdirect).

This article provides general information only. It is not a diagnosis or individual treatment advice. Speak with a qualified health professional if you need advice for your circumstances.

Why driving can leave your neck feeling stiff

Driving asks you to hold a fairly fixed posture while making small, repeated movements to check mirrors, traffic and blind spots. A seat that is too far from the wheel may encourage you to reach forwards, while a reclined backrest can leave your head sitting in front of your shoulders. Prolonged poor posture is one recognised contributor to neck pain (Mayo Clinic).

Tension can build if you grip the steering wheel firmly or lift your shoulders without realising it. Previous neck trouble, reduced movement and the general strain of a long trip may also influence how you feel afterwards. Pain by itself does not identify which joint, muscle or other structure is responsible, so avoid trying to diagnose a “disc problem” from the location of the ache alone.

What to do when you get out of the car

Start by walking around for a few minutes rather than immediately sitting on the sofa. Then try comfortable neck movements: slowly look left and right, bring one ear towards each shoulder, and gently look up and down. Keep the range modest and stop before a movement becomes sharp, dizzying or produces spreading symptoms. Staying active and using gentle neck exercises are commonly advised for uncomplicated neck pain (healthdirect).

Heat can be pleasant when the main problem is stiffness. A warm shower or wrapped heat pack may be used for a short period, taking care not to burn the skin or fall asleep with a heat pack in place. Cold packs are another option, particularly if the area feels recently irritated; both heat and cold appear in standard self-care guidance for neck pain (Mayo Clinic).

Avoid forcefully twisting your neck or repeatedly trying to make it crack. If movement is painful, keep it easy and return to it later rather than pushing through. Over-the-counter pain medicines may suit some people, but they are not safe for everyone; a pharmacist or GP can check for issues such as other medicines, pregnancy, stomach ulcers, kidney disease or allergies (healthdirect).

Adjust the car before the next drive

Sit with your hips well back in the seat and bring the seat close enough that you can hold the wheel without reaching or locking your elbows. Set the backrest fairly upright while keeping it comfortable. The head restraint should sit behind your head rather than down near the upper back, as correctly positioned head restraints help reduce neck movement during a crash (NSW Government).

  • Move the mirrors after settling into your normal driving position, so you do not need to crane forwards to use them.
  • Let your shoulders drop and use a light, controlled grip on the wheel.
  • Keep frequently used items within safe reach, but never reach for something while the vehicle is moving.
  • Take regular breaks on longer journeys. Get out somewhere safe, walk briefly and change position.

Do not attempt neck stretches while driving. Your attention and control of the vehicle come first. If lower back discomfort is also making it difficult to sit comfortably, this guide to driving and practical lumbar disc management discusses seat position, walking and activity in more detail.

The evening after the trip and the next day

Normal daily movement is generally preferable to prolonged bed rest for simple neck pain (Better Health Channel). A short walk, light household activity and gentle movement may be reasonable if they do not clearly aggravate your symptoms. Heavy lifting, sustained overhead work and long periods looking down at a phone can wait if they are uncomfortable.

For sleep, choose a position that keeps your neck reasonably neutral and feels settled. There is no single pillow height that suits everybody because body shape, mattress firmness and sleeping position differ. Avoid sleeping on your stomach if it forces your head to remain fully turned and that position is provoking the pain.

If the stiffness is not beginning to settle, keeps returning after driving or limits your ability to turn your head safely, arrange an assessment. A clinician may check neck movement, strength, reflexes and sensation, then advise whether exercises, hands-on care, medication review or further investigation are appropriate. Imaging is not automatically needed for every episode of neck pain and is usually considered in the context of the history and examination (Mayo Clinic).

Symptoms that need medical attention

Seek urgent medical care if neck pain follows a significant collision or other trauma, or if it comes with new weakness, numbness, poor coordination, trouble walking, loss of bladder or bowel control, fever, a severe headache or feeling seriously unwell. Neck pain accompanied by chest pain, shortness of breath, sweating or nausea also needs urgent assessment. Australian guidance recommends prompt evaluation where neck pain is severe, follows injury or occurs with neurological or other concerning symptoms (healthdirect).

After a crash, symptoms such as neck pain, reduced movement, headache or arm symptoms may relate to whiplash and should be assessed appropriately (healthdirect). Call Triple Zero (000) for a medical emergency. If you are uncertain how urgently you need care, you can contact healthdirect on 1800 022 222 for advice from a registered nurse.

Frequently asked questions

These are general answers. Your medical history, the circumstances of the drive and any associated symptoms may change what is appropriate.

Should I stretch a stiff neck straight after driving?

Gentle, comfortable movement is usually a better starting point than a strong stretch. Move slowly and stop if you develop sharp pain, dizziness, numbness or symptoms spreading into an arm. Staying active and trying gentle exercises are included in Australian self-care guidance for neck pain (healthdirect).

Is heat or ice better for neck stiffness?

Either may be tried, depending on which feels more soothing. Wrap the pack, protect your skin and use it for a limited period; standard neck pain guidance includes both heat and cold as self-care options (Mayo Clinic).

Can I drive again if my neck is still sore?

Only drive if you can control the vehicle safely and check traffic, mirrors and blind spots properly. Do not drive if pain, restricted movement, dizziness, weakness or sedating medicine could impair you. Seek professional advice if you are unsure.

How long should I wait before having it checked?

Arrange an assessment sooner if the pain is severe, follows an accident, spreads into an arm or is accompanied by weakness, numbness, fever, severe headache or problems with balance. For milder symptoms, seek advice if they are worsening, not starting to improve or repeatedly returning after drives (healthdirect).
