Wash Dishes With Lower Back Pain: Practical Tips

22 November 2026 · Andy Lee, Chiropractor · Lower back pain

The sink is full after dinner, and within a few minutes your lower back starts to ache. If you need to wash dishes with lower back pain, the usual problem is not the weight of a plate. It is spending too long leaning slightly forward, reaching into the sink and standing in one position.

You do not need a perfect posture. A more useful aim is to make the task adjustable: bring items closer, change position often and stop before discomfort becomes difficult to settle. These ideas can be used in most Australian kitchens without buying special equipment.

Why washing up may irritate a sore back

A kitchen sink often sits just low enough to encourage a small forward bend. Add wet dishes, reaching and several minutes of standing, and the position can become tiring. This does not prove that you have damaged your back. Lower back pain can have several possible causes, and the intensity of pain does not reliably identify one particular structure (Healthdirect).

Notice what happens rather than forcing yourself to hold a supposedly ideal stance. Some people dislike bending forward. Others are more bothered by standing still. If forward bending is a regular trigger, this guide to lower back pain when bending forward covers the issue in more detail.

How to wash dishes with lower back pain

Start by standing close enough that you are not reaching across the bench. Keep the dishes you are about to wash beside the sink, rather than behind you. Turn your feet and whole body towards each item instead of repeatedly twisting from the waist.

Try these options and keep the ones that feel easier:

  • Soften your knees. They do not need to be deeply bent. Just avoid locking them while you stand.
  • Use a wider or staggered stance. Place one foot a little ahead of the other, then swap sides every few minutes.
  • Rest one foot on a low, stable support. A solid step may be useful if it does not create a trip hazard. Change feet regularly.
  • Bring your body closer. Light contact between your thighs or hips and a dry bench edge can provide some support, provided the surface is secure and comfortable.
  • Move at your hips as well as your back. If you need to lean, let your hips move backwards slightly rather than rounding forward and reaching with your shoulders.

None of these is a rule you must hold throughout the job. The best position is often the next comfortable one. Regularly changing your stance is usually more practical than trying to stay rigid.

Set up the sink before turning on the tap

A minute of preparation can remove a surprising amount of reaching. Scrape plates first, stack similar items together and place frequently used things within easy reach. If the sink is deep, use a washing-up bowl or smaller tub if that brings the work closer to you. It must sit firmly and should not become too heavy to empty safely.

Place the drying rack on your preferred side. If you normally twist to put each plate behind you, move the rack beside the sink instead. Keep detergent and brushes near the front of the bench. For a large saucepan, let it soak and wash it separately rather than holding it away from your body while scrubbing.

Be careful with a wet floor. Clean up splashes promptly and wear footwear that feels stable. If you use a foot support, choose something designed to bear weight rather than an upside-down container or loose stack of objects.

Use shorter batches and keep moving

You do not have to finish every item in one go. Wash for a few minutes, put several things away, then return later. A short walk around the kitchen or a gentle change of position may be enough to avoid becoming stuck in one posture. Clinical guidelines for uncomplicated lower back pain generally favour continuing ordinary activity as tolerated rather than prolonged rest (American College of Physicians guideline).

If discomfort starts building, pause before it becomes severe. Stand upright, walk briefly or sit down if that settles things. There is no need to push into sharp pain to strengthen your back. A small amount of temporary discomfort may be manageable, but pain that keeps escalating, spreads down a leg or changes how you can stand deserves more attention.

The same pacing approach can help with other household work. Vacuuming involves longer reaches and more repeated movement, so the setup is a little different. See how to vacuum with lower back pain for practical adjustments.

When back pain needs to be assessed

Arrange a health assessment if the pain is persistent, keeps returning, is limiting normal activity or is accompanied by symptoms in a leg. An examination can help clarify whether your current movements need modifying and whether exercise or other care is appropriate. Exercise can help some people with ongoing non-specific lower back pain, although the suitable type and starting level vary between individuals (Cochrane review indexed by PubMed).

Seek urgent medical help if back pain follows significant trauma, or occurs with new bladder or bowel control problems, numbness around the genitals or anus, marked leg weakness, fever, or feeling seriously unwell. Healthdirect lists these as reasons for prompt medical assessment (Healthdirect). Call Triple Zero on 000 if symptoms appear life-threatening.

Frequently asked questions

Can I lean against the kitchen bench?

Yes, if the bench is dry, stable and the edge is comfortable. Light contact can give you another position to use, but avoid pressing painfully into the area or leaning on a surface that could move.

Would sitting down to wash dishes help?

It can be an option if you can sit close to the sink without twisting or reaching upwards. Use a stable chair or perching stool, keep the floor clear and avoid carrying a full tub of water while seated.

Is heat useful after washing up?

A warm pack may provide temporary comfort for some types of back pain. Protect your skin, keep the temperature comfortable and do not use heat on an area with reduced sensation (Healthdirect).

Should I stop household chores until the pain is gone?

Complete rest is not usually advised for uncomplicated lower back pain. It is generally better to keep up tolerable activity, using smaller batches and easier variations while symptoms settle (American College of Physicians guideline).
