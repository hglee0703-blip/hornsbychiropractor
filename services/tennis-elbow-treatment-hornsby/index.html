Elbow pain

Tennis elbow and golfer's elbow care in Hornsby

Tennis elbow causes pain on the bony outside of the elbow, often when gripping, lifting with the palm facing down or hitting a backhand. Golfer's elbow causes similar pain on the inner side. Despite the names, both can follow repeated gripping and lifting at work or at home, not only sport (Mayo Clinic) (Mayo Clinic).

At our Hornsby clinic we assess the elbow, forearm, wrist and neck, explain what the findings suggest, and discuss suitable care or referral.

Who we see

Elbow problems people commonly book for

  • Pain on the outside of the elbow when gripping, lifting a kettle or turning a handle
  • Pain on the inner elbow with gripping, throwing or golf
  • Elbow pain that started with racquet sport or a return to sport
  • Elbow and forearm pain linked with mouse, keyboard or tool use
  • Elbow pain with neck stiffness or arm symptoms (see neck pain care)

Sudden increases in repetitive load, such as more playing time, a new tool or a busy period at work, are recognised contributors to tennis elbow (The Lancet, 2015).

Assessment

What happens at your appointment

We ask when the pain started, which grips and movements provoke it, and what has changed in your work, home tasks or sport. The examination checks the elbow, forearm and wrist, grip and how the tendons respond to load, and the neck, because neck problems can also refer pain into the arm.

Imaging is not usually needed for a typical presentation. If the history or examination suggests another problem, we discuss referral to your GP (Mayo Clinic).

Care options

Care options we may discuss

Most tennis elbow can be managed without surgery, although recovery can take time (Mayo Clinic). Clinical guidance for lateral elbow pain supports progressive exercise for the wrist and forearm muscles, adjusted to how irritable the elbow is (JOSPT, 2022).

Depending on the findings, a plan may include hands-on treatment for the elbow, forearm and neck, graded strengthening, and practical changes to grip, tools, workstation or training load. A forearm strap may make some activities more comfortable, but it does not replace graded loading.

Corticosteroid injections can reduce pain in the short term, but a randomised trial found less favourable longer-term outcomes and more recurrence than placebo, so discuss the benefits and drawbacks with your GP (JAMA, 2013). Before treatment we discuss expected benefits, risks, alternatives and fees.

Before booking

When to seek prompt medical care

  • A fall or impact followed by deformity, severe swelling or inability to move the elbow
  • Marked weakness, or numbness that is spreading in the arm or hand
  • A hot, red, swollen elbow, especially with fever

These features are not typical of tennis elbow and need prompt medical assessment (Mayo Clinic).

Questions

Common questions about tennis elbow care

Should I stop all activity?

Usually not. Reduce the grips and tasks that clearly provoke the pain, keep other activity going, and rebuild gradually. Recovery can take time and varies between people (Mayo Clinic).

Do I need an injection?

Not necessarily. Injections may ease pain in the short term but have drawbacks over the longer term (JAMA, 2013). Your GP can discuss whether one suits you.

Can I have tennis elbow if I don't play tennis?

Yes. The name describes the pain pattern, not the cause. Repetitive gripping and lifting at work or at home can produce the same problem (Mayo Clinic).
