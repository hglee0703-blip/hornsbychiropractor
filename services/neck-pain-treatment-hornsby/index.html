Neck pain

Neck pain and stiffness care in Hornsby

Neck pain and stiffness can follow long hours at a desk, an awkward night's sleep, a minor strain or a long drive. Sometimes the neck also sends pain, pins and needles or numbness into the shoulder or arm.

At our Hornsby clinic we assess how your neck moves and what reproduces your symptoms, explain the findings, and discuss suitable care or referral.

Who we see

Neck problems people commonly book for

  • Pain or stiffness when turning the head
  • Waking with a sore, stiff neck
  • Neck and shoulder tension linked with computer work or phone use
  • Neck pain that spreads into an arm with tingling or numbness (see disc pain and arm symptoms)
  • Headaches that seem to start in the neck (see headache care)
  • Ongoing neck pain after a whiplash injury, once serious injury has been ruled out

Assessment

What happens at your appointment

We ask how the pain began, which movements or positions reproduce it, whether it travels, and how it affects work, sleep and driving. The examination checks neck and upper back movement and, when arm symptoms are present, strength, sensation and reflexes.

Age-related changes on an X-ray or MRI are common and do not always cause symptoms, so scan findings are interpreted alongside your examination (Mayo Clinic). We explain what we find and whether medical review is needed.

Care options

Care options we may discuss

Depending on the findings and your preferences, options may include hands-on care such as joint mobilisation or manipulation and soft-tissue techniques, graded exercise, and practical changes to your desk, phone use or sleeping position. Treatment should be matched to the clinical findings rather than applied the same way to everyone (PubMed).

Neck manipulation carries risks. Temporary soreness, headache or tiredness can occur, and rare serious complications, including a type of stroke, have been reported (Mayo Clinic). We discuss the risks relevant to you, the alternatives and fees before any treatment.

For uncomplicated neck pain, comfortable movement and a gradual return to normal activity are generally preferred to keeping the neck still for long periods (healthdirect).

Before booking

When to seek urgent medical care

Seek urgent medical care rather than booking a chiropractic appointment if you notice:

  • Weakness, numbness or clumsiness in the arms or hands that is getting worse
  • Difficulty walking, or new problems with bladder or bowel control
  • Fever with a stiff neck, or a sudden severe headache
  • Pain after a fall, car accident or other significant injury
  • Neck or jaw pain with chest pain, sweating or breathlessness
  • Sudden facial drooping, slurred speech, vision loss or one-sided weakness

Pressure on the spinal cord in the neck can affect coordination, walking and bladder function (Mayo Clinic). Neck or jaw pain with chest discomfort can be a sign of a heart attack (healthdirect), and sudden one-sided weakness or speech changes can be a sign of stroke (healthdirect). Call 000 for these symptoms.

Questions

Common questions about neck pain care

Should I rest my neck until the pain settles?

Long periods of keeping the neck still are not usually recommended for uncomplicated neck pain. Gentle, comfortable movement and a gradual return to normal activities are generally preferred (healthdirect). Stop a movement that causes spreading arm pain, numbness or weakness.

Do I need an X-ray before my appointment?

Not usually. Imaging decisions depend on your history, examination and any warning signs (Mayo Clinic). If you already have scan reports, bring them along.

Can my neck be treated without manipulation?

Yes. Manipulation is only one option. If you prefer not to have it, or it is not suitable for you, other approaches such as mobilisation, soft-tissue techniques and exercise can be discussed.
