Heel pain

Plantar fasciitis and heel pain care in Hornsby

Plantar fasciitis commonly causes pain near the bottom of the heel. It is often worst with the first steps after getting out of bed or after sitting for a while (Mayo Clinic).

Similar heel pain can occasionally come from other problems, so at our Hornsby clinic we assess how and where your heel hurts, explain the findings, and discuss suitable care or referral.

Who we see

Heel problems people commonly book for

  • Sharp heel pain with the first steps in the morning
  • Heel or arch pain after standing all day at work
  • Heel pain that started with running, walking more or a change of shoes
  • Heel pain that keeps returning or changes how you walk

Plantar fasciitis is more common between the ages of 40 and 60, in people whose work keeps them on their feet, with running and some other types of exercise, with certain foot shapes, and with excess body weight (Mayo Clinic).

Assessment

What happens at your appointment

We ask when the pain started, when it is worst, and what has changed in your activity, work or footwear. The examination looks at the tender area, ankle and foot movement, calf flexibility, and how you stand and walk.

Imaging is not routinely needed for a straightforward presentation, but may be used when another condition is suspected (Mayo Clinic).

Care options

Care options we may discuss

Clinical guidance for heel pain supports conservative care that adjusts activity and includes exercise, rather than relying on one treatment (PubMed). Plantar fascia-specific stretching (PubMed) and progressive heel-raise strengthening (PubMed) have both been studied as options.

Depending on the findings, a plan may include hands-on treatment for the foot, ankle and calf, a stretching and strengthening programme, and advice on footwear, standing and walking load. Most people improve with conservative treatment, although it can take several months (Mayo Clinic).

Before treatment we discuss expected benefits, risks, alternatives and fees. If another option such as orthotics, an injection or a medical review may help, we discuss referral.

Before booking

When to seek prompt medical care

  • Heel or foot pain after a significant injury, or if you cannot bear weight
  • A hot, red, markedly swollen foot, or foot pain with fever
  • Numbness, a wound that is not healing, or new foot symptoms if you have diabetes

These need prompt medical advice rather than a chiropractic appointment (healthdirect).

Questions

Common questions about plantar fasciitis care

Why does my heel hurt most in the morning?

Pain with the first steps after sleep or sitting is a typical feature of plantar fasciitis (Mayo Clinic). It often eases as you start moving, then may return after long periods on your feet.

Should I stop walking or running?

Complete rest is not usually needed. Reduce the amount that clearly aggravates the heel, keep a comfortable baseline of activity and build up gradually (PubMed).

Do I need new shoes or orthotics?

Supportive, well-fitting shoes help some people, and orthotics may be considered for others (Mayo Clinic). We can look at your footwear during the assessment.
