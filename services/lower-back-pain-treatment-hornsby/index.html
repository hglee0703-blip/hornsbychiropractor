Lower back pain

Lower back pain care in Hornsby

Lower back pain can start after lifting, gardening or a long day of sitting, or appear without an obvious cause. Most back pain is not caused by a serious condition, but pain that limits work, sleep or exercise deserves a proper assessment (healthdirect).

At our Hornsby clinic we assess your symptoms, movement and daily activities, then discuss suitable care, an individual plan or referral where appropriate.

Who we see

Lower back problems people commonly book for

  • Pain when standing up after sitting or after long periods of sitting
  • Pain when bending forward or lifting
  • Morning stiffness or pain when rolling over in bed
  • Back pain when walking or returning to exercise
  • Back pain during pregnancy, alongside advice from your maternity care team
  • Back pain that spreads into a leg (see disc pain and sciatica care)

Assessment

What happens at your appointment

We take a careful history, examine movement and everyday function, check for signs of a more serious cause and, when leg symptoms suggest nerve involvement, test strength, sensation and reflexes. This reflects the assessment described in Australia's Low Back Pain Clinical Care Standard.

Imaging is not routinely needed for low back pain. It is considered when the history or examination gives a reason, such as concern about a serious underlying cause (Low Back Pain Clinical Care Standard).

Care options

Care options we may discuss

Staying active within comfortable limits is generally preferred to bed rest for uncomplicated back pain (healthdirect), and exercise can help some people with ongoing non-specific low back pain (Cochrane).

Depending on the assessment, a plan may combine hands-on chiropractic treatment with graded exercise, advice on sitting, lifting and sleep, and a gradual return to work, sport or gardening. Before treatment we discuss the expected benefits, risks, alternatives and fees, and how progress will be reviewed.

Before booking

When to seek urgent medical care

Call 000 or go to an emergency department rather than booking a chiropractic appointment if you have:

  • New loss of bladder or bowel control, or difficulty passing urine
  • Numbness around the genitals, bottom or inner thighs
  • Weakness in one or both legs that is getting worse
  • Back pain after a significant fall or accident

The first three can occur with cauda equina syndrome, which needs emergency treatment (healthdirect). Back pain with fever, unexplained weight loss or a history of cancer also needs prompt medical assessment (Mayo Clinic).

Questions

Common questions about lower back pain care

Should I rest in bed until my back settles?

Long periods of bed rest are not usually recommended. Keep moving within comfortable limits and build activity gradually (healthdirect). Seek advice if pain spreads into a leg or you notice numbness or weakness.

How many appointments will I need?

That depends on your assessment and how you respond. We discuss a proposed plan, how progress will be reviewed and the expected costs, and you can change or stop care at any time.

Should I see my GP first?

You can book directly without a referral. If the assessment suggests medical investigation, medicines advice or another health professional would help, we will discuss referral to your GP.
