Our central focus

Disc pain and sciatica care in Hornsby

Disc-related neck and lower back pain is a central focus at Hornsby Chiropractor. People often book after being told they have a bulging, protruding or herniated disc, or when pain travels from the lower back into a leg (sciatica) or from the neck into an arm.

We assess your symptoms, movement and nerve function, explain what the findings suggest, and discuss whether conservative care is suitable or whether medical review should come first.

Who we see

Symptoms people commonly book for

  • Lower back pain that spreads into the buttock or down a leg
  • Neck pain with pain, pins and needles or numbness in an arm or hand
  • Pain that makes sitting, standing up, bending or walking difficult
  • A scan report that mentions a disc bulge, protrusion, herniation or degeneration
  • Ongoing pain after discussing injections or surgery with a doctor

Sciatica describes pain that follows the sciatic nerve from the lower back through the buttock and down the leg. It most often happens when a herniated disc or a bony narrowing puts pressure on part of the nerve (Mayo Clinic).

A scan finding does not explain pain on its own. Disc bulges and degeneration also appear on imaging in many people without back pain, so we interpret any report alongside your history and examination (PubMed).

Assessment

What happens at your appointment

We start with how the pain began, where symptoms travel, and which positions or activities make them better or worse. The examination looks at spinal movement and, when nerve involvement is suspected, strength, sensation and reflexes in the arms or legs.

We then explain the findings in plain language, including what remains uncertain. If the picture suggests a problem that needs medical investigation, we will tell you and discuss referral to your GP.

Please bring any scan reports, specialist letters and a list of your medicines. You do not need to arrange new imaging before booking.

Care options

Conservative care we may discuss

When conservative care is appropriate, a plan may combine hands-on chiropractic treatment with advice about sitting, driving, sleeping and staying active, and exercises chosen according to how your symptoms respond to testing. For some lower back presentations this includes McKenzie-style repeated movements. An exercise that helps one disc problem can aggravate another, so it is selected and reviewed individually.

Before treatment we discuss the expected benefits, risks, alternatives and fees. You can ask questions, decline a technique or stop care at any time.

If surgery has been recommended, we can discuss whether conservative care has a role alongside your specialist's advice. Chiropractic care must not delay surgery or medical care that you need, and no one can promise that surgery will be avoided.

For more detail, read our guides to day-to-day lumbar disc management and disc protrusion, sciatica, injections and surgery.

Before booking

When to seek urgent medical care

Do not wait for a chiropractic appointment if you notice any of the following. Call 000 or go to an emergency department:

  • New difficulty passing urine, or loss of bladder or bowel control
  • Numbness around the genitals, bottom or inner thighs
  • Weakness in a leg or arm that is getting worse quickly
  • Back or neck pain after a fall, car accident or other significant injury
  • Pain with fever, or feeling very unwell

These symptoms can signal serious nerve compression or another condition that needs medical treatment (healthdirect). See also healthdirect's information on spinal disc problems.

Questions

Common questions about disc pain care

Do I need a referral or a scan before booking?

No. You can book a private appointment without a GP referral or new imaging. If you are claiming through a scheme such as workers compensation, check what paperwork it needs. Imaging is not routinely needed for uncomplicated back pain and is considered when the history or examination gives a reason (healthdirect).

Can I see a chiropractor if a surgeon has recommended surgery?

Yes, you can book an assessment to talk through conservative options. We will ask about your specialist's advice and any timelines. Care is not a substitute for surgery when it is needed, and individual outcomes vary.

Is it safe to keep moving with a disc problem?

For uncomplicated back pain, staying active within comfortable limits is generally preferred to long periods of bed rest (healthdirect). Stop and seek advice if an activity brings on spreading pain, new numbness or weakness.
