Shoulder pain

Frozen shoulder and shoulder pain care in Hornsby

Frozen shoulder, also called adhesive capsulitis, causes pain and stiffness that gradually limit how far the shoulder can move. It usually develops slowly, worsens over time and then eases, which can take a long time (Mayo Clinic).

Other shoulder problems, such as rotator cuff irritation or pain referred from the neck, can look similar at first. We assess your shoulder and neck, explain what the findings suggest and discuss suitable care or referral.

Who we see

Shoulder problems people commonly book for

  • A shoulder that is becoming stiff and painful, especially reaching up or behind the back
  • Shoulder pain that disturbs sleep, particularly lying on that side
  • Pain when reaching overhead
  • Neck and shoulder pain linked with carrying bags or swimming

Frozen shoulder is more common in people aged 40 and over, in women, in people with diabetes, and after the shoulder has been kept still following an injury or surgery (Mayo Clinic). Rotator cuff problems can also cause a dull ache, disturbed sleep and difficulty reaching (Mayo Clinic).

Assessment

What happens at your appointment

We ask how the pain started, how it has changed, and which movements and positions are difficult. The examination compares how far you can move the shoulder yourself with how far it moves with assistance, tests strength, and checks the neck, because neck problems can refer pain into the shoulder.

If imaging, blood tests or a medical opinion would help clarify the picture, we will discuss referral to your GP.

Care options

Care options we may discuss

Depending on the findings, options may include hands-on treatment for the shoulder, neck and upper back, gentle range-of-motion exercises, and advice on sleeping positions and daily tasks. Some people with frozen shoulder also need medical options, such as medicines or injections, which your GP can discuss.

Different shoulder problems can need different care, so the plan is reviewed as your symptoms change. Before treatment we discuss the expected benefits, risks, alternatives and fees.

Before booking

When to seek urgent medical care

  • Shoulder, arm or jaw pain with chest pain, sweating or breathlessness: call 000 (healthdirect)
  • A fall or injury with severe pain, deformity or inability to lift the arm
  • A hot, swollen, very painful joint, especially with fever (healthdirect)
  • Sudden weakness or numbness in the arm

These symptoms need medical assessment before chiropractic care is considered.

Questions

Common questions about shoulder care

How can I tell if it is frozen shoulder?

Frozen shoulder typically causes a gradual loss of movement in many directions, even when someone else tries to move the arm for you. Rotator cuff problems and pain referred from the neck can feel similar, so an examination is needed.

Should I keep moving a painful shoulder?

Gentle movement within a comfortable range is usually encouraged, but forcing a stiff shoulder through sharp pain is not. Ask which movements suit your stage of symptoms.

How long does frozen shoulder last?

It often develops and eases slowly, over months or longer (Mayo Clinic). Timelines vary between people. We review your progress and discuss referral if it is not changing as expected.
